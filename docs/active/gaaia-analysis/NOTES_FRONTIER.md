# FRONTIER Act notes — running list

Working list for outreach on the FRONTIER Act (introduced 2026-07-23, H.R. number TBD — text-as-posted still `H.R. ll`). Same three-tier structure as `NOTES.md` (GAAIA): (1) drafting bugs, zero-cost to accept; (2) cheap technical asks, small text changes with outsized effect; (3) substantive asks, Canary comparative advantage. Provenance: 2026-07-24 ingest (`bills/frontier-act/`, 9 per-section summaries + `FRONTIER_VS_GAAIA.md`) + same-day walkthrough with Dan. Seeded from the walkthrough; the full walk of GAAIA `NOTES.md`'s 31 items against FRONTIER is still pending and will grow this list.

## Tier 1 — drafting bugs

1. **§ 4(h)(2) routing bug, carried verbatim from GAAIA § 111(g)(2).** 24-hour imminent-risk-of-death reports go to "a law enforcement agency with jurisdiction," not the Under Secretary. A local PD or FBI field office has neither AI-incident capacity nor a statutory duty to forward. Flagged to the GAAIA inbox in June; survived the rewrite unchanged. Fix: route to the Under Secretary AND law enforcement, or add a forwarding duty.

## Tier 2 — cheap technical asks

1. **§ 3(f): add "or decrease" — the threshold ratchet points backwards.** The Under Secretary may only *raise* the compute/revenue/expenditure thresholds, even though § 3(f)(2)'s calibration standard explicitly cites "advances in algorithmic and hardware efficiency." Efficiency gains move catastrophic-risk capability DOWN the compute curve; the only fast lever points up. If the executive-rulemaking design is about keeping pace with the technology (plausible), the one-way direction contradicts that rationale in exactly the dangerous direction. Textually a two-word fix; politically heavier (executive discretion over coverage), so pair with the § 3(f)(3) biennial-review publication as the check.
2. **§ 3: restore the consultation clause.** GAAIA § 111(k)(3) / § 112(p)(3) required stakeholder consultation (academia, open-source community, private/public sector) before rulemaking; FRONTIER § 3 requires none. One subsection restores it.
3. **§ 4(e): restore GAAIA § 111(d)'s false-statements scope.** GAAIA barred knowing falsity about catastrophic risk, its management, AND framework compliance; § 4(e) reaches only subjects the transparency report must cover. Framework-compliance falsity now bites only through the § 4(c) audit backstop.
4. **§ 6: express the audit/assessment cumulation intent.** Default reading is that very-large developers owe BOTH the § 4(c) compliance audit and the § 5 IVO assessment (coherent — different questions), but no provision says so expressly and the "except as otherwise expressly provided" carve-out is empty. One sentence forecloses the litigation.
5. **§ 7: pair the GAO report with a mandatory response window.** GAO documenting IVO capture compels nothing. The Under Secretary already HAS authority to act (license revocation, § 3(c) COI rules) — the gap is obligation, not power; the SEC/Big Four and pre-2008 credit-rating history is capture documented for years while authority sat unused. Ask: statutory duty for the Under Secretary to respond within X days to any GAO finding of systemic capture.

## Tier 3 — substantive asks

1. **Restore § 113 whistleblower protections as a new § 10.** GAAIA's whistleblower title — anti-retaliation, jury trial, non-waivable rights incl. mandatory-arbitration override, whole-industry coverage, no sunset — is dropped without replacement (zero hits for "whistle"/"retaliat" in FRONTIER). Under the new architecture the loss compounds: § 4(c) auditors are developer-picked and developer-paid, § 5 IVOs are developer-paid, and an IVO lead partner who signs a hostile report or pulls the § 5(p)(1)(B) tripwire has no statutory anti-retaliation floor either. There is no channel for the employee who sees what the auditor and IVO miss. ~4-line insertion of GAAIA § 113 text. Lead ask.
2. **Inseverability clause for the core trade (§§ 4/5/9), carried from GAAIA Tier 2.8 — now more urgent.** Still no severability or inseverability language anywhere in the bill. Default doctrine presumes severability, so a compelled-speech challenge (NetChoice playbook) striking §§ 4–5 leaves § 9's preemption standing alone — and with the sunset gone, that failure mode is now *permanent* naked preemption rather than a 3-year one. States would have no federal regime AND no power to build their own. One-sentence fix linking the substantive duties to the preemption.
3. **§ 8 intake gap: no statutory trigger below the very-large tier.** § 8's emergency authority reaches ANY frontier developer (no revenue floor — good), but the only mandatory feeder is the § 5(p)(1)(B) IVO tripwire, which covers ~5 companies. Below that tier, § 4(h) incident reports go to the Under Secretary with no statutory escalate-to-§ 8 pathway, and nothing obligates the Secretary to act on over-the-transom information. The authority is broadest exactly where its intake plumbing is thinnest. Ask options: a § 4(h) escalation duty (Under Secretary must refer qualifying incidents to the Secretary on a clock), or extend a lightweight referral duty to § 4(c) auditors.
4. **Audited-artifact provenance, carried from GAAIA Tier 3 unchanged.** Neither § 4(c) audit nor § 5 IVO assessment binds the assessed weights to the served weights — checkpoint substitution (assess A, ship A′) remains undetectable by any record the statute requires. Same ask as before: registered fingerprint of evaluated weights, auditor hash certification, tamper-evident derivation records. Standard post-SolarWinds federal-vendor compliance stack; marginal burden ~zero.

## Observed, not asking (for now)

- **§ 2(18)(B) equity-loss carve-out from "property."** Defensible: without it, ordinary market carnage (incl. AI-driven volatility generally) would trip the $1B gate constantly. Residual concern: a targeted incident whose primary damage IS equity destruction (market manipulation, a hack that craters a specific company) has its main harm excluded, leaving only physical damage to clear $1B. Watch; don't lead with it.
- **§ 8(l) exclusivity / Commerce-only architecture.** Likely deliberate command-simplification. Cuts both ways: prevents federal-agency freelancing on catastrophic-risk grounds, but also creates a single point of refusal (a Secretary who declines to act cannot be routed around), and as a constraint on an erratic President it is thin — the Secretary serves at pleasure. Interagency deconfliction (CISA/FBI/DOD roles in a live incident) is unaddressed anywhere in the bill; that piece may be worth an ask later.
