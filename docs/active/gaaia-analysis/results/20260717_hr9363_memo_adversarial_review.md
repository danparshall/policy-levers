## Inference 1: "impossible as drafted" — refuted; narrow it

**The refutation.**

1. **The amendment executes.** GAAIA §102(a)(2) instructs "in title LIII... by adding at the end the following new section." That instruction executes literally even if a §5304 already exists — the result is two sections designated 5304, a duplicate designation, not a failed amendment. Duplicate section numbers occur in the U.S. Code; the Office of the Law Revision Counsel annotates them and later technical-corrections acts renumber (that is where alpha-suffixed sections like "5304a" come from). Ugly, not impossible.
2. **"Every dependent cross-reference breaks" is false.** GAAIA Title I references route through the §101(9) definition of "Director," which is anchored *self-disambiguatingly*: "under section 5304 of the [NAIIA], **as added by section 102 of this Act**." That anchor points at GAAIA's own section regardless of a rival §5304. Grepping the full text, GAAIA contains exactly **two** numbered cross-references to NAIIA §5304 — §101(9) and §112(q)(3) — plus the clerical TOC item. (The third `5304` hit, in the Title II pay provision, is 5 U.S.C. §5304 locality pay — unrelated.) The renumbering cure is four edits: §102's section designation, the TOC item, §101(9), §112(q)(3). "Cross-references its §5304 throughout" overstates by an order of magnitude.
3. **"Repeal-and-replace of a just-enacted section" is one path, not the path.** Renumber-and-coexist is textually available (two centers, one measurement-voluntary and sunsetting in 5 years, one regulatory). The real cost of 9363-first is political — an incumbent center to displace and a 29–0 committee vote to argue against — not a drafting impossibility.

**What the memo missed — the collision that actually misfires.** Both bills amend NAIIA §5002 (15 U.S.C. 9401) with incompatible instructions:

- GAAIA §102(a)(1): amends para (3) heading (Artificial → AI; artificial), strikes "term 'artificial intelligence' means" / inserts plural, redesignates (4)–(11) as **(9)–(16)**, inserts new (4) AI interpretability, (5) adversarial robustness (leaving (6)–(8) vacant).
- 9363 §2(a)(1): amends para (3) heading *identically*, strikes the *same* "means" text, redesignates (4)–(11) as **(6), (8)–(11), (13)–(15)**, inserts new (4) AI model, (5) AI system, (7) foreign adversary, (12) intelligence community.

Whichever enacts second, its strike instructions find no text to strike (the first bill already struck it) and its redesignation instructions hit paragraphs that have moved or that the first bill newly created — a genuine execution failure/misfire, not a duplicate designation. Still curable by Legislative Counsel in minutes, but this is the *concrete, checkable* reconciliation problem for the Obernolte/Foushee ask, and stronger evidence of shared drafting DNA than the TOC bug (the two bills' §5002 edits are visibly the same base text, forked).

**Bonus finding (GAAIA-internal bug, annex candidate):** §112(q)(3) cites "section 5304(c)(1)(X)(vii) of the [NAIIA], **as added by section 111**" — §5304 is added by §102, not §111. Mis-anchored cross-reference in the very clause the memo cited as its example.

## Inference 2: use-immunity constrains a later GAAIA — refuted as law; narrow to transition + optics

**The refutation.**

1. **Rules of construction cannot entrench.** 9363's (i), (l)(1)–(3), and (e)(2)(B) govern the construction and operation of *that section under current law*. No Congress can bind a later Congress by ordinary statute; a later-enacted GAAIA's express mandatory-reporting and enforcement provisions operate of their own force (later-in-time; express provisions need no "strip"). A `notwithstanding` clause would be cheap belt-and-suspenders, not a legal necessity.
2. **Scope: it's a use restriction, not immunity from regulation.** (e)(2)(B) restricts use of "information shared with or provided to the Director... to carry out subsection (c)" — i.e., the voluntary-sharing channel. It does not immunize the entity's *activities*. GAAIA's machinery runs on its own compelled submissions under §§111–112, which never pass through 9363's (c) channel. Harmonization is trivial, so implied-repeal disfavor never even bites: there is no conflict to repeal.
3. **The killer fact: GAAIA has the same clause.** GAAIA §102(d)(2)(B) — new §5304(d)(2)(B) — provides that information shared with *its* Center pursuant to subsection (c) "may not... be used by any Federal, State, local, or Tribal government to regulate an activity of such entity with respect to which such information relates." Materially identical to 9363's (e)(2)(B). The memo's framing ("statutory use-immunity — inverse of GAAIA's mandatory-report-and-act CAISI") is wrong on its face: it is GAAIA's own clause, copied (more shared-DNA evidence). GAAIA coexists with it because compelled §§111–112 reports are not "pursuant to subsection (c)" voluntary sharing. The same harmonization saves a later GAAIA from 9363's version.

**What survives (the narrow, defensible residue).**

- **Transition-period argument:** information a lab *already* shared with the 9363 Center pre-GAAIA carries a colorable (e)(2)(B) + fair-notice argument against being used in later enforcement, absent an express override. Real, but small: GAAIA would regulate from its own compelled record.
- **Politics/optics:** enact use-immunity for voluntary sharers, then mandate reporting months later — reads as bait-and-switch and will be argued as one. This is ammunition GAAIA's opponents get if 9363 passes first; it constrains *votes*, not *validity*.

**New flags surfaced by this pass:**

- **9363 sunset eats its own confidentiality promise.** (k) terminates "this section" 5 years post-enactment — on a plain reading, the (e)(1) FOIA exemption and (e)(2) use/disclosure protections for *already-shared* information terminate with it. Industry-friendly offices should care that the confidentiality promise has an expiry date. (Also: (j) authorizes appropriations FY2027–2032, six fiscal years, under a five-year sunset — the tail year funds a terminated section.)
- **GAAIA-internal tension (flag, not conclusion):** GAAIA §102(c)(1)(F) has the Center "analyzing audit and assessment reports submitted under section 112." If those reports are deemed "provided to the Center... pursuant to subsection (c)," §102(d)(2)(B)'s use-restriction could be stretched against §111/§112 enforcement referrals. Best reading: compelled §112 submissions are not (c)-channel sharing, and referral-for-enforcement is arguably not "regulat[ing]"; but a clarifying sentence in GAAIA would foreclose the argument. Annex candidate.

## Inference 3: ceiling verdict vs (a)(3) — survives

The (a)(3) transfer study does not puncture "ceiling as drafted":

1. **Discretionary twice over** — "The Secretary *may* conduct a study, *as the Secretary determines appropriate*." No mandate, no deadline, no trigger.
2. **Output is paper.** The study yields an assessment plus "[r]ecommendations for Congress" on authority and funding, with 30-day committee submission. It grants no authority and no funding.
3. **It adds nothing.** Agencies recommend legislation to Congress as a matter of course, and 9363's own (c)(3) already gives the Director a direct assessment-and-recommendation channel to NSC and OSTP. The hatch is decorative.
4. **Expressio unius cuts the memo's way.** Drafters contemplated growth and expressly reserved it to a future act of Congress — which *confirms* that the enacted section confers none. The memo's own formulation ("growth contemplated, but only via a future act of Congress") was already correct and survives attack.

**One scope caveat, inherited from inference 2:** "ceiling" is true of (a) 9363's own architecture — (i) no regulatory/rulemaking/enforcement authority, (l) locks, voluntary-only (c), Director-defined thresholds (c)(4)/(m)(2), $20M, 15-slot direct-hire cap, sunset — and (b) its political function, occupying the CAISI slot with a center whose enabling text forbids GAAIA's duties. It is **not** a legal cap on what a later Congress may enact. Blog and Hill language should never imply that 9363 *legally blocks* GAAIA; it *textually collides with* and *politically preempts* it.
