# AI Open Letters, Statements & Declarations Inventory (2023–July 2026): A Tracker for Policymaker Outreach

Scan run 2026-08-12 in response to Dan's question of which AI-related open letters/statements/petitions exist beyond the Global Statement on AI Risk, wemustactnow.ai, and aitreaty.org, and whether documenting them for policymakers would be valuable.

## TL;DR
- Since GPT-4's March 2023 release, at least 30 distinct AI-related open letters/statements/petitions have emerged, clustering into five waves and nine categories (x-risk, governance, ethics/faith, labor, copyright, open-source, military, child safety, direct action); the two most consequential by reach are the FLI "Pause" letter (~30,000+ signatories, March 2023) and the FLI "Statement on Superintelligence" (October 2025, 700+ high-profile signers, 32,214 total signatories by late October 2025).
- A small set of "super-signers" — above all Yoshua Bengio, Geoffrey Hinton, Stuart Russell, Max Tegmark, and Steve Wozniak — appear across nearly every x-risk/governance letter; the same names sometimes appear on letters with contradictory operational implications (most notably Elon Musk, who signed the 2023 Pause letter while incorporating xAI on March 9, 2023).
- The best existing curated resources are AI Lab Watch's open-letters list, FLI's own open-letters page, and Georgetown ETO's AGORA governance database — but none is a comprehensive, signatory-level tracker across ALL categories, which is the gap this inventory fills.

## Key Findings
1. **Two organizations dominate the x-risk letter ecosystem**: the Future of Life Institute (FLI) and the Center for AI Safety (CAIS). FLI authored the 2023 Pause letter and the 2025 Superintelligence Statement; CAIS authored the 2023 one-sentence extinction statement.
2. **Signatory counts accumulate over time** and differ by source and date — always cite the count with its date. The Pause letter was reported at ~27,565 (May 8, 2023) and later "more than 30,000" (FLI, March 2024). The Superintelligence Statement launched at "more than 700 individuals" (October 22, 2025) and reached 32,214 signatories within days (late October 2025).
3. **The creative/copyright cluster is numerically the largest single-issue mobilization** outside the x-risk petitions: the Ed Newton-Rex "Statement on AI Training" hit 11,500+ within days (October 2024) and had surpassed 50,000 signatories by early 2026.
4. **Positions are polarized on regulation**: pro-regulation letters (SB 1047 support, red lines, treaty calls) coexist with strong anti-regulation/pro-open-source mobilizations (a16z/YC opposition to SB 1047, the EU "Stop the Clock" letter, Mozilla's openness statement).
5. **Waves map to focusing events**: GPT-4 release (Mar 2023), the UK AI Safety Summit (Nov 2023), SB 1047 (mid-2024), the UN General Assembly (Sept 2025), and AI-driven layoffs (2026).

## The Inventory

### Category A — Existential / Catastrophic Risk

**1. Pause Giant AI Experiments ("the Pause letter")**
- Date: March 22, 2023. Sponsor: Future of Life Institute.
- Ask: Immediately pause for at least 6 months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4.
- Signatories: reported ~27,565 (as of May 8, 2023, per IEEE Spectrum), later "more than 30,000" (FLI, March 2024). Signatures were initially unverified after an embargo break; FLI later added email verification.
- Notable: Yoshua Bengio, Stuart Russell, Elon Musk, Steve Wozniak, Yuval Noah Harari, Andrew Yang, Emad Mostaque (Stability AI), Connor Leahy (Conjecture), Jaan Tallinn, Max Tegmark, Gary Marcus.
- Category: x-risk / pause. URL: futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments/
- Impact: Enormous coverage (NYT, BBC, WaPo, etc.); credited with generating "renewed urgency within governments" but the pause never happened. Full signatory list public/scrapeable.

**2. Statement on AI Risk (CAIS extinction statement)**
- Date: May 30, 2023. Sponsor: Center for AI Safety.
- Ask (full text): "Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war."
- Signatories: 700+ (CAIS), some sources "over 1,000"; 100+ AI professors at launch.
- Notable: Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Sam Altman (OpenAI), Dario Amodei (Anthropic), Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind), Bill Gates, Ilya Sutskever, Dawn Song, Ya-Qin Zhang. Meta leadership notably did NOT sign.
- Category: x-risk. URL: aistatement.com / safe.ai/statement-on-ai-risk
- Impact: Historic coalition; heavily covered. Critics (Timnit Gebru, Émile Torres, Human Rights Watch) argued it distracts from present harms and could serve regulatory capture. Full list public.

**3. Managing AI Risks in an Era of Rapid Progress**
- Date: October 26, 2023 (later published in Science, 2024, as "Managing extreme AI risks amid rapid progress").
- Ask: A consensus academic paper outlining extreme risks and urgent R&D + governance priorities.
- Authors: 25 named, incl. Bengio, Hinton, Andrew Yao, Dawn Song, Pieter Abbeel, Yuval Noah Harari, Ya-Qin Zhang, Gillian Hadfield, Stuart Russell, Daniel Kahneman.
- Category: x-risk / governance (authored paper, not mass-signature). URL: managing-ai-risks.com / arXiv 2310.17688. Full author list public.

**4. Urging an International AI Treaty**
- Date: October 30/31, 2023 (eve of UK AI Safety Summit). Sponsor: aitreaty.org, led by Tolga Bilge.
- Ask: Development and ratification of an international AI treaty with global compute thresholds, a "CERN for AI safety," safe APIs, and a compliance commission.
- Notable: Yoshua Bengio, Yi Zeng, Max Tegmark, Gary Marcus, Bart Selman, Victoria Krakovna, Luke Muehlhauser.
- Category: governance / treaty. URL: aitreaty.org. Full list public. (Note: aitreaty.org later became a member of the "Coalition for a Baruch Plan for AI.")

**5. Statement on Superintelligence**
- Date: October 22, 2025. Sponsor: Future of Life Institute.
- Ask (full text): "We call for a prohibition on the development of superintelligence, not lifted before there is broad scientific consensus that it will be done safely and controllably, and strong public buy-in."
- Signatories: "more than 700 individuals" at launch (TIME/FedScoop, Oct 22, 2025), growing to 32,214 signatories within days (per Zvi Mowshowitz's LessWrong analysis, late October 2025). (Some higher circulating figures were not corroborated.)
- Notable: Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Stuart Russell, Steve Wozniak, Richard Branson, Prince Harry & Meghan (Duke & Duchess of Sussex), Steve Bannon, Glenn Beck, Susan Rice, Mike Mullen (former Joint Chiefs chair), Mary Robinson, Stephen Fry, Joseph Gordon-Levitt, will.i.am, Yuval Noah Harari, five Nobel laureates, Paolo Benanti (papal AI adviser).
- Category: x-risk / prohibition. URL: superintelligence-statement.org
- Impact: Major coverage; notable for an ideologically diverse ("nerds vs. nerds" gone "mainstream," per Tegmark) coalition. Criticized by Dean Ball (Foundation for American Innovation) as unoperationalizable. Full list public.

**6. Global Call for AI Red Lines**
- Date: September 22, 2025 (launched at 80th UN General Assembly). Sponsors: CeSIA (French Center for AI Safety), The Future Society, and CHAI (UC Berkeley).
- Ask: Governments to agree by end of 2026 on binding international "red lines" for AI (e.g., banning bioweapon design, mass surveillance, AI impersonation).
- Signatories: "more than 200 prominent politicians and scientists, including 10 Nobel Prize winners" at the UNGA launch (NBC News/Wikipedia); red-lines.ai now lists 746 total signatories, described as "300+ prominent figures... and 90+ organizations."
- Notable: Announced by Nobel Peace laureate Maria Ressa; signers include Bengio, Hinton, Joseph Stiglitz, Daron Acemoglu, Mary Robinson, Juan Manuel Santos, Enrico Letta, Csaba Kőrösi, Wojciech Zaremba (OpenAI), Ian Goodfellow (DeepMind), Jason Clinton (Anthropic CISO), Yuval Noah Harari, Audrey Tang, Stephen Fry.
- Category: governance / red lines. URL: red-lines.ai
- Impact: Timed to UNGA; per Transformer News (Sept 2025) it "is unlikely to move the needle on concrete governance, largely due to American opposition," citing Sen. Ted Cruz's push for Congress to "counter excessive foreign regulation" and the Trump AI Action Plan. Full list public.

**7. IDAIS — International Dialogues on AI Safety consensus statements**
- IDAIS-Ditchley (UK, Oct 2023); IDAIS-Beijing "Consensus Statement on Red Lines in Artificial Intelligence" (March 10–11, 2024); IDAIS-Venice "Consensus Statement on AI Safety as a Global Public Good" (Sept 2024); plus a later IDAIS (Oxford/Royal Society).
- Sponsor: Safe AI Forum (a FAR.AI project) with Beijing Academy of AI (BAAI) for the Beijing edition.
- Ask (Beijing): Five red lines — no autonomous replication/improvement, no power-seeking, no assisting WMD development, no autonomous cyberattacks, no deception; plus domestic registration of large training runs. Signed by 24 experts.
- Notable: Turing winners Bengio, Hinton, Andrew Yao; Fu Ying (former Chinese Vice Foreign Minister); Xue Lan (Tsinghua); Hongjiang Zhang, Huang Tiejun (BAAI); Gillian Hadfield; Stuart Russell; Mary Robinson.
- Category: x-risk / US-China track-II governance. URL: idais.ai
- Significance: One of few English-language forums bridging Chinese and Western researchers. Full signatory lists public.

**8. The Elders + FLI joint letter (long-term risks)**
- Date: February 14, 2024. Sponsors: The Elders and FLI.
- Ask: Decision-makers to address climate crisis, pandemics, nuclear weapons, and "ungoverned AI." Signatories: 2,672 (per FLI). Category: governance / multi-risk. URL: futureoflife.org.

### Category B — Governance / Regulation (US and EU)

**9. SB 1047 support letter — AI company employees**
- Date: September 9, 2024. Ask: Urged Gov. Newsom to sign California's SB 1047.
- Signatories: at least 113 current and former employees of OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Anthropic, Meta, and xAI.
- Category: governance / pro-regulation. Impact: Part of pressure campaign; bill vetoed Sept 29, 2024.

**10. SB 1047 support — "Artists 4 Safe AI" / Hollywood letter**
- Date: September 24, 2024. Sponsor: Artists for Safe AI. Ask: Urged Newsom to sign SB 1047.
- Signatories: 125+ entertainment-industry figures.
- Notable: J.J. Abrams, Shonda Rhimes, Judd Apatow, Ava DuVernay, Mark Hamill, Jane Fonda, Mark Ruffalo, Pedro Pascal, Alec Baldwin, Mahershala Ali, Joseph Gordon-Levitt, Jessica Chastain, plus SAG-AFTRA's Fran Drescher and Duncan Crabtree-Ireland.
- Category: governance / labor-creative. Impact: High media profile; bill vetoed.

**11. SB 1047 opposition — Y Combinator / startup founders letter**
- Date: June 2024. Sponsor: Y Combinator. Ask: Opposed SB 1047; claimed (inaccurately, per Sen. Wiener and Brookings) developers could face jail. Signed by dozens of startup founders incl. YC CEO Garry Tan. Category: anti-regulation / open-source.

**12. SB 1047 opposition — a16z and coalition letters**
- Multiple letters/statements (mid-2024) from Andreessen Horowitz (chief legal officer Jaikumar Ramaswamy), plus a coalition letter organized via the International Center for Law & Economics (signers incl. ALEC Action, American Consumer Institute, Center for Data Innovation). Academic opposition from UC and Caltech researchers. Category: anti-regulation. Impact: Contributed to veto.

**13. EU AI Act "Stop the Clock" open letter**
- Date: July 3, 2025. Sponsor: EU AI Champions Initiative (coalition convened by Jeannette zu Fürstenberg, General Catalyst).
- Ask: A two-year "clock-stop" pause on AI Act obligations for GPAI (Aug 2025) and high-risk systems (Aug 2026).
- Signatories: ~45–50 major European companies incl. Airbus, ASML, BNP Paribas, Carrefour, Dassault Systèmes, Lufthansa, Mercedes-Benz, Mistral, Philips, Siemens Energy, TotalEnergies, Black Forest Labs, ElevenLabs.
- Category: anti-regulation / industry. Impact: European Commission rejected it ("no stop the clock, no grace period, no pause," spokesperson Thomas Regnier). Full list public.

**14. EU AI Act — earlier open letters (both directions)**
- April 2023: DAIR et al. "Five considerations to guide the regulation of General Purpose AI in the EU's AI Act." Nov 2023: "EU AI Act Open Letter" (tieredapproach.eu). A June 2023 letter from European executives also warned the Act could harm competitiveness. Category: governance, mixed.

**15. Biden Executive Order 14110 supporting statements**
- Oct 30–31, 2023. Various industry "what they are saying" endorsements (Business Roundtable, AI security firms). Note: EO 14110 was itself a government action, not an open letter; revoked by EO 14148 (Jan 20, 2025).

**16. US state-legislator letters against federal AI moratorium**
- June 3, 2025: bipartisan letter from 260 state lawmakers (all 50 states) opposing a proposed 10-year federal moratorium on state AI regulation, organized with Americans for Responsible Innovation; led by SC Rep. Brandon Guffey and SD Sen. Liz Larson. Plus ~40 state AGs and 17 GOP governors opposing. Outcome: Senate stripped the moratorium 99–1 (July 1, 2025).
- Follow-on (June 17, 2026): 203 legislators (bipartisan) opposing a renewed preemption push (GAAIA); 36 state AGs via NAAG (Nov 25, 2025). Category: governance / anti-preemption.

### Category C — Whistleblowing / Developer Concerns

**17. A Right to Warn about Advanced Artificial Intelligence**
- Date: June 4, 2024. Sponsor: righttowarn.ai (current/former frontier-lab employees).
- Ask: AI companies to end non-disparagement agreements, create anonymous channels for raising concerns, support open criticism, and not retaliate against whistleblowers.
- Signatories: 13 (11 current/former OpenAI, 2 Google DeepMind), several anonymous; endorsed by Bengio, Hinton, Stuart Russell.
- Notable named: Daniel Kokotajlo, Jacob Hilton, William Saunders, Carroll Wainwright, Daniel Ziegler, Ramana Kumar, Neel Nanda.
- Category: developer / whistleblower. Impact: High-profile; followed OpenAI's disbanding of its superalignment team and non-disparagement controversy.

### Category D — Copyright / Creative Workers / Labor

**18. Authors Guild Open Letter to Generative AI Leaders**
- Date: July 18, 2023. Sponsor: The Authors Guild.
- Ask: OpenAI, Alphabet, Meta, Stability AI, IBM, Microsoft to obtain consent, credit, and fairly compensate writers for use of copyrighted work.
- Signatories: 8,000+ at launch, grew to 15,000+.
- Notable: Margaret Atwood, James Patterson, Jodi Picoult, Jennifer Egan, George Saunders, Michael Chabon, Jonathan Franzen, Nora Roberts, Suzanne Collins.
- Category: copyright / labor. Open for public signing.

**19. Statement on AI Training (Ed Newton-Rex / "aitrainingstatement.org")**
- Date: October 22, 2024. Organizer: Ed Newton-Rex (Fairly Trained).
- Ask (full text): "The unlicensed use of creative works for training generative AI is a major, unjust threat to the livelihoods of the people behind those works, and must not be permitted."
- Signatories: 11,500+ within days (NBC, Oct 22, 2024); European Writers Council reported "more than 19,000" two days after launch; "more than 50,000 people" per Newton-Rex's King's College London bio (Feb 2026).
- Notable: All five members of Radiohead (Thom Yorke), ABBA's Björn Ulvaeus, Robert Smith (The Cure), Kazuo Ishiguro, Kate Mosse, James Patterson, Julianne Moore, Kevin Bacon, Rosario Dawson, Kate McKinnon; org backers incl. Universal, Sony, Warner Music, SAG-AFTRA (Fran Drescher), Artist Rights Alliance, Concept Art Association, Human Artistry Campaign.
- Category: copyright / creative. Impact: Major leverage in UK "opt-out" debate. Full list public.

**20. Artists' letters against generative AI (visual arts)**
- The Concept Art Association and Artists Rights Society-linked campaigns (2023–2024); Concept Art Association was a key backer of California AB 2013 (training-data disclosure). Multiple class actions (Andersen v. Stability AI). Category: copyright / creative.

**21. SAG-AFTRA video game strike & AI statements**
- Strike authorized Sept 2023; began July 26, 2024; suspended June 11, 2025; new Interactive Media Agreement ratified July 9, 2025 (95.04% yes).
- Ask: AI digital-replica consent, disclosure, compensation, and a "strike suspension" (anti-scab) provision for all voice and motion-capture performers.
- Category: labor. Impact: Won consent/disclosure/usage-report terms plus a 15.17% pay increase; bolstered by California AB 2602 & AB 1836. Chief negotiator Duncan Crabtree-Ireland; negotiating-committee chair Sarah Elmaleh.

**22. We Must Act Now: A Statement on AI's Transformation of the Economy**
- Date: July 13, 2026. Sponsor: Stanford Digital Economy Lab (organizers Erik Brynjolfsson, Ajay Agrawal, University of Virginia's Anton Korinek, and METR researcher Tom Cunningham).
- Ask (88-word statement): Economists, policymakers, and tech leaders must "act now" to build incentives, guardrails, and institutions as AI may transform the economy faster than the Industrial Revolution, risking large-scale job displacement.
- Signatories: "more than 200 economists and AI researchers... including sixteen Nobel Laureates" (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, July 13, 2026); one press account cited 1,649 at the time of writing.
- Notable: Daron Acemoglu, Simon Johnson, Joseph Stiglitz, Paul Krugman, Ben Bernanke, George Akerlof, Michael Spence, David Autor, Jason Furman, Gita Gopinath; tech: Yoshua Bengio, Yann LeCun, Eric Schmidt, Reid Hoffman, Jeff Dean, Jack Clark (Anthropic co-founder), Ronnie Chatterji (OpenAI chief economist), Vinod Khosla.
- Category: labor / economic. URL: wemustactnow.ai
- Significance: Notable because Acemoglu and Autor had previously downplayed AI displacement fears — a shift in the economics profession. Full list public/signable.

### Category E — Ethics / Faith

**23. Rome Call for AI Ethics**
- Original signing Feb 28, 2020 (Vatican/Pontifical Academy for Life, Microsoft, IBM, FAO, Italian government); Abrahamic-religions signing Jan 10, 2023 (Christianity, Judaism, Islam); Hiroshima "AI Ethics for Peace" July 9–10, 2024 added Eastern religions (Buddhism, Hinduism, Zoroastrianism, Bahá'í, etc.), described as 11 world religions with 16 new signatories.
- Ask: Six principles ("algorethics") — transparency, inclusion, responsibility, impartiality, reliability, security/privacy — ensuring AI serves humanity.
- Notable: Archbishop Vincenzo Paglia, Fr. Paolo Benanti (later on UN AI advisory body), Brad Smith (Microsoft), Dario Gil (IBM); corporate signers later incl. Cisco, Qualcomm.
- Category: ethics / faith. URL: romecall.org.

**24. Southern Baptist / Evangelical AI statements**
- ERLC "Artificial Intelligence: An Evangelical Statement of Principles" (April 11, 2019; ~65–70 evangelical leaders incl. J.D. Greear, Wayne Grudem, Russell Moore, Andrew T. Walker) — pre-dates window but foundational.
- SBC Resolution "On Artificial Intelligence and Emerging Technologies" (June 13–14, 2023) — first denominational AI resolution; centers human dignity.
- Category: ethics / faith. URLs: erlc.com; sbc.net.

### Category F — Open-Source / Openness

**25. Mozilla Joint Statement on AI Safety and Openness**
- Date: October 31, 2023. Sponsor: Mozilla (open.mozilla.org/letter).
- Ask: Argues openness/transparency (incl. open-source models) is a tool for AI safety, not a risk; pushback against restricting open models. Category: open-source advocacy. Full list public.

**26. Meta and civil-society openness statements**
- Meta "Statement of Support for Meta's Open Approach" (July 2023); "Openness and Transparency in AI Provide Significant Benefits for Society" (CDT-hosted civil-society letter, March 25, 2024, for the NTIA open-weights process). Category: open-source.

### Category G — Military / Autonomous Weapons

**27. Autonomous weapons letters (ongoing)**
- The foundational FLI-hosted "Autonomous Weapons Open Letter" (2015; 3,000+ AI/robotics researchers + 17,000 others) and the 2017 companies' letter remain active reference points. The Campaign to Stop Killer Robots maintains ongoing pledges and an interfaith "A Plea for Preserving Our Shared Humanity."
- IDAIS red lines and the Global Call for AI Red Lines both name autonomous-weapons/loss-of-control prohibitions.
- Note: Most flagship autonomous-weapons letters pre-date 2023; the theme now runs through the red-lines and treaty letters. Category: military.

### Category H — Grassroots / Direct Action

**28. PauseAI** (founded May 2023, Joep Meindertsma) — "PauseAI Statement/Proposal" calling for a pause on frontier training until provably safe, plus an IAEA-style international agency and global treaty; known for protests across 13+ countries. In one action, 60 UK politicians signed a PauseAI letter to Demis Hassabis alleging a breach of the Frontier AI Safety Commitments. URL: pauseai.info.

**29. StopAI** (stopai.info) — more radical; demands "a permanent and enforceable global ban on the further development of frontier AI technology." Non-violent direct action at OpenAI HQ.

**30. Encode Justice + FLI — "AI Licensing for a Better Future"**
- Date: October 25, 2023. Ask: Support a tiered federal AI licensing regime (à la Blumenthal-Hawley) addressing both present harms (bias, discrimination) and catastrophic risks. Encode Justice describes itself as ~900 young people. Category: governance.

### Category I — Child Safety (emerging)

**31. Parent/child-safety mobilization around AI chatbots**
- Backing the GUARD Act (S.3062, introduced Oct 28, 2025, Hawley-Blumenthal et al.) to ban AI companion chatbots for minors; supported by ParentsSOS, RAINN; testimony from parents of teens (Sewell Setzer/Character.AI; Adam Raine/ChatGPT). Common Sense Media advocacy. Category: ethics / child safety.

## Cross-Cutting Analysis

### The "Super-Signers"
- **Yoshua Bengio** and **Geoffrey Hinton** are the two most ubiquitous signatories, appearing on: Pause (Bengio), CAIS Statement (both), Managing AI Risks (both), Right to Warn endorsers (both), IDAIS (both), Global Call for AI Red Lines (both), Statement on Superintelligence (both), We Must Act Now (Bengio). They are the connective tissue of the entire x-risk/governance coalition.
- **Stuart Russell** — Pause, Managing AI Risks, Right to Warn, IDAIS, Superintelligence Statement.
- **Steve Wozniak** — Pause and Superintelligence Statement (the two big FLI mass letters).
- **Max Tegmark / Anthony Aguirre** — architects (FLI) behind Pause, Superintelligence, and treaty-adjacent efforts.
- **Yuval Noah Harari** — Pause, CAIS, Managing AI Risks, Red Lines, Superintelligence.
- **Yann LeCun** is a revealing NON-signer of x-risk letters but DID sign the economic "We Must Act Now" letter — marking the boundary between the "doomer" and "accelerationist" camps.

### Contradictions
- **Elon Musk** signed the March 2023 Pause letter while incorporating xAI (March 9, 2023) and recruiting staff — the starkest example of a signer acting against the letter's spirit. He also backed SB 1047.
- **Sam Altman / Dario Amodei / Demis Hassabis** signed the CAIS extinction statement, yet their companies drove the very frontier race the Pause and Superintelligence letters sought to halt; none signed the Superintelligence Statement.
- **Mistral** signed the EU "Stop the Clock" anti-regulation letter, yet CEO Arthur Mensch had recently downplayed regulation as Europe's problem — and Mistral also signed the EU GPAI Code of Practice.
- **Joseph Gordon-Levitt, Fran Drescher, SAG-AFTRA** appear across the creative-copyright statement, the SB 1047 Hollywood letter, and the Superintelligence Statement — a genuine bridge between the labor/creative cluster and the x-risk cluster.
- **Daron Acemoglu / David Autor** signed "We Must Act Now" (2026) despite years of downplaying AI job-loss fears — a notable reversal.

### Coalition Clusters
1. **The x-risk/governance core** (FLI + CAIS + IDAIS + Red Lines + treaty): heavily overlapping; academic + some lab leadership.
2. **The creative/labor cluster** (Authors Guild, AI Training Statement, SAG-AFTRA, Artists 4 Safe AI): overlaps with x-risk only via a few celebrity bridge-signers and via the Superintelligence Statement's celebrity contingent.
3. **The economics cluster** ("We Must Act Now"): distinct, Nobel-economist-led, overlapping with x-risk only through Bengio/LeCun/Schmidt.
4. **The anti-regulation/open-source cluster** (a16z, YC, Mozilla, EU "Stop the Clock," Meta): oppositional to cluster 1; almost no signatory overlap.
5. **The faith/ethics cluster** (Rome Call, evangelical statements): largely separate institutional actors; Paolo Benanti bridges into the Superintelligence Statement.

### Timeline / Waves
- **Wave 1 — Spring/Summer 2023 (post-GPT-4):** Pause (Mar), CAIS Statement (May), Authors Guild (Jul). Trigger: GPT-4.
- **Wave 2 — Fall 2023 (pre-UK Summit):** Managing AI Risks, AI Treaty, Mozilla openness, Encode/FLI licensing, EU AI Act letters, CHAI China-West statement, IDAIS-Ditchley (all Oct–Nov 2023). Trigger: Bletchley Summit.
- **Wave 3 — 2024:** IDAIS-Beijing (Mar), Right to Warn (Jun), SB 1047 support/opposition letters (Jun–Sep), IDAIS-Venice (Sep), AI Training Statement (Oct). Triggers: SB 1047, OpenAI safety exits.
- **Wave 4 — 2025:** EU "Stop the Clock" (Jul), state-legislator anti-moratorium letters (Jun), Global Call for AI Red Lines (Sep), Statement on Superintelligence (Oct), GUARD Act mobilization (Oct). Triggers: UNGA, US federal preemption fight.
- **Wave 5 — 2026:** We Must Act Now (Jul), renewed anti-preemption letters (Jun). Trigger: AI-driven layoffs.

### Geographic / Institutional Breakdown
- **US-centric:** SB 1047 letters, Right to Warn, Authors Guild, We Must Act Now, state-legislator letters, child-safety.
- **European:** EU "Stop the Clock," EU AI Act letters, aitreaty.org (UK-led).
- **China-inclusive (rare):** IDAIS-Beijing, CAIS Statement (Chinese signatories), Global Call for AI Red Lines (Ya-Qin Zhang, Huang Tiejun).
- **Global/faith:** Rome Call (interfaith, Vatican-led).

## Policy Impact vs. Media Flash
- **Real/traceable impact:** SB 1047 letters (shaped the veto debate and Anthropic's amendments); Right to Warn (contributed to OpenAI reversing non-disparagement clawbacks); AI Training Statement (leverage in UK opt-out debate); EU "Stop the Clock" (rejected outright, but signaled industry pressure feeding later simplification efforts); state-legislator letters (Senate stripped the moratorium 99–1); SAG-AFTRA (won concrete contract AI terms).
- **Mostly agenda-setting / media impact:** Pause letter (no pause materialized), CAIS Statement (discourse-shaping), Superintelligence Statement (mainstreaming the debate), Red Lines (no binding treaty yet; hampered by US opposition), We Must Act Now (too recent to assess).

## Existing Trackers / Databases
- **AI Lab Watch — Open Letters** (ailabwatch.org/resources/open-letters): the single best curated chronological list of AI-safety open letters and inter-country declarations.
- **FLI Open Letters page** (futureoflife.org/open-letters): curated collection of FLI-run letters with verified signatory lists.
- **Georgetown ETO AGORA** (agora.eto.tech): 1,000+ AI governance documents with plain-English summaries and tags — focused on laws/regulations more than advocacy letters.
- **CAIS AI Safety Newsletter**, **MIRI**, **NCSL AI Legislation Database** (state bills), **Stanford HAI AI Index**, **MIT AI Risk Repository** — adjacent resources, none a comprehensive signatory-level open-letter tracker. This confirms the value of building a dedicated cross-category tracker.

## Recommendations
1. **Prioritize the six flagship letters for policymaker briefings**: Pause (2023), CAIS Statement (2023), Right to Warn (2024), Global Call for AI Red Lines (2025), Statement on Superintelligence (2025), We Must Act Now (2026). These have the highest name recognition and coalition breadth. Benchmark: if any of these gains sitting-government or frontier-lab-CEO signatures, escalate it in briefings as a policy-viability step-change.
2. **Build the tracker's signatory database around the "super-signer" spine** (Bengio, Hinton, Russell, Tegmark, Harari, Wozniak) and flag cross-letter contradictions (Musk, Mistral, lab CEOs). This overlap-and-contradiction layer is the highest-value analytical output for policymakers.
3. **Scrape now, because counts move**: Pause, Superintelligence (32,214 as of late Oct 2025), AI Training Statement (50,000+ by early 2026), Red Lines (746), and We Must Act Now are all open for continued signing; capture counts with timestamps and re-scrape quarterly.
4. **Watch these thresholds**: a passed US federal AI law would trigger a new wave of implementation-focused letters; a major lab publicly signing a prohibition/red-lines letter would be a coalition-defining event.
5. **Fill gaps via direct outreach** to CeSIA/The Future Society (Red Lines), FLI (Pause, Superintelligence), and the Authors Guild for authoritative signatory exports; ETO for structured governance-document data.

## Gaps: Topics Without a Major Open Letter (Yet)
- **AI in healthcare/clinical decision-making** — no flagship multi-stakeholder open letter despite rapid adoption.
- **AI in journalism** — publisher lawsuits (NYT v. OpenAI) but no unified open-letter mobilization.
- **AI in education** — no signature-driven statement comparable to the creative-sector one.
- **AI and the environment/energy (datacenter demand)** — emerging concern, no flagship letter.
- **AI in financial systems / systemic risk** — absent.
- **Chinese-language domestic letters** — English-language visibility limited to IDAIS; likely under-captured.

## Caveats
- **Signatory counts are moving targets** and vary by source; every count in this inventory should be cited with its observation date. Early Pause-letter signatures were unverified after an embargo break.
- **Government declarations** (Bletchley Declaration Nov 2023, Seoul Ministerial Statement May 2024, Council of Europe Framework Convention Sept 2024) are inter-state instruments, not civil-society open letters, and are listed here only for context.
