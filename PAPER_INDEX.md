# Paper Index

## Research Papers

| Paper | Description | File |
|-------|-------------|------|
| Armstrong, Bostrom & Shulman (2013) | FHI game-theoretic model of an AI arms race: n teams choose safety s vs capability c; team with highest (c−s) wins and survives with probability s. Nash-equilibrium disaster risk falls with capability-importance and rises with team count, enmity, and — counterintuitively — with more shared information about capabilities. Canonical statement of the "AI arms race" framing | `Armstrong_Shulman__2013--racing_to_precipice.pdf` |
| Askell, Brundage & Hadfield (2019) | OpenAI paper framing responsible AI development as a **collective action problem** (not merely competition): market forces, liability, and regulation are all systematically weaker for frontier AI than for pharma/aviation, and competitive pressure pushes labs to under-invest below what they themselves would prefer. Five game-theoretic cooperation factors (High Trust, Shared Upside, Low Exposure, Low Advantage, Shared Downside) and four strategy families (correct misconceptions, joint research, appropriate oversight, adherence incentives incl. domain-specific ones like early-compute-access). Explicit downstream of Armstrong/Bostrom/Shulman 2013; the theoretical scaffold Anthropic's WABI 2026 "verifiable coordinated slowdown/pause" proposal sits on. Notable Hadfield author cross-link: same author on Sastry/Heim/Belfield 2024 (compute governance) and Hadfield & Clark 2023 *Regulatory Markets* (the acknowledged single largest inspiration for Ball 2025). Three of this collection's entries thread through her | `Askell_Hadfield__2019--cooperation_responsible_ai.pdf` |
| Naudé & Dimitri (2020) | All-pay contest refinement of the Armstrong-Bostrom-Shulman 2013 AGI-race model: teams choose R&D investment (not safety effort) subject to a competition coefficient ρ = a/c − b combining technology ceiling, unit cost, and R&D difficulty. Central result — winner-takes-all + costly R&D + hard problem endogenously selects a small number of racers ("half a dozen or so"), partially answering Armstrong's "too many teams" concern. Second-prize / intermediate-outcome extension shows public procurement can raise both entry and effort; friendliness-graded tax and AI-patent reform are additional model-derived policy levers | `Naude_Dimitri__2020--race_for_agi_public_policy.pdf` |
| Jones (2024) | Formal growth-vs-existential-risk model; argues society underinvests in AI safety by ~30x | `Jones_2024__AI_dilemma_growth_vs_risk.pdf` |
| Kording & Marinescu (2025) | Nested-CES "intelligence saturation" model bridging AI-expert and economist growth forecasts | `Kording_2025__AI_saturation_future_of_work.pdf` |
| Mertens, Thompson et al. (2026) | MIT FutureTech large-scale O*NET task evaluations; "rising tide" vs METR's "crashing wave" | `Mertens_2026__crashing_waves_rising_tides_AI_automation.pdf` |
| METR (2025) | 50%-task-completion time horizon benchmark; doubling every ~7 months | `METR_2025__long_tasks_frontier.pdf` |
| Parshall & Lopez-Luzuriaga (2026) | Task exposure framework (author's working paper) | `Parshall_Lopez-Luzuriaga_TaskExposure_2026.pdf` |
| Rahman (2026) | MIRI simulation of governance-evading distributed training: Llama-405B-scale local-equivalent compute on 625 sub-monitoring nodes (~$441M) over consumer internet; Scher/EU-AI-Act/SB-53 thresholds evadable for $1.6M/$31M/$3.8B in unmonitored hardware; chip tracking + whistleblowing effective, bandwidth caps + traffic monitoring not | `RahmanR__2026--distributed_training_compute_governance.pdf` |
| Zhou, Hernández-Orallo et al. (2025) | Psychometrics-inspired AI evaluation: 18 continuous demand scales replace aggregate benchmark accuracy; interpretable per-instance success predictor beats black-box baselines OOD; explicit sandbagging/overestimation/underestimation warnings for regulators | `ZhouL_Hernandez-Orallo__2025--general_scales_ai_evaluation.pdf` |
| MacDonald (2021) | MIT NSE thesis "Hide and Seek": quantitative model of space-based-radar tracking of TELs; coverage-gap search area grows ~(vt)²; finds evasive operation + countermeasures defeat near-term tracking. Superset of the JSS article, incl. radar-physics chapter | `MacDonaldT__2021--hide_and_seek_remote_sensing_strategic_stability.pdf` |
| MacDonald (2025) | "Tracking Mobile Missiles," J. Strategic Studies 48(2) 297-333: article version of the thesis; space radar necessary but current gaps (tens of min) preclude custody; dozens of dedicated radar sats required; commercial-sensor conglomeration unlikely to matter. Counterpoint to Lieber & Press 2017 | PDF Cloudflare-blocked; DOI: [10.1080/01402390.2024.2435961](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01402390.2024.2435961) (open access) |
| Ortega (2025) | Apollo Research proposal for a legally-mandated post-deployment AI incident regime focused on national security; three phases (Preparatory / Rapid Response / Hardening Defenses) modeled on NRC / NTSB / NIH-FSAP; novel incident operationalization = "any event weakening a claim in the AI system's pre-deployment national security case," argued superior to OECD's serious-AI-hazard definition; 24-hour notification, government intelligence-gathering + security-strengthening authorities. Direct technical parent for FRONTIER Act § 111/§ 112 architecture | `OrtegaA__2025--ai_incident_regime_national_security.pdf` |
| IOSCO (2025) | Consultation Report CR/01/2025 (March 2025) from the International Organization of Securities Commissions' Fintech Task Force AI Working Group: cross-jurisdictional survey (24/33 IOSCO members + 6 SROs + 184 AMCC respondents) mapping AI use cases in capital markets, four risk-category taxonomy (malicious use / model & data / concentration & third-party dependency / human-AI interaction), and inventory of existing-frameworks vs bespoke-frameworks regulatory approaches across HK, EU, Canada, US-CFTC, Greece, Japan, Brazil, Australia. Phase-1 of a two-phase workplan; Phase 2 (per IOSCOPD789) develops recommendations. Comment period closed 11 Apr 2025 | `IOSCO__2025--ai_in_capital_markets.pdf` |
| Aumann & Lindell (2007) | TCC 2007 paper introducing the **covert-adversary** threat model in secure multiparty computation: parties who will deviate from a protocol if it lets them cheat, but only if they're not caught. Formal security definition indexed by a "deterrence factor" ε (probability an honest party detects a cheat), with efficient two-party protocols. Foundational threat-modeling frame — the "cheats-if-uncaught" adversary generalizes cleanly to AI-safety concerns about behavior that differs under observation vs deployment (deceptive alignment) and to governance regimes that rely on audit + penalty rather than continuous prevention | `AumannY_LindellY__2007--security_against_covert_adversaries.pdf` |
| Sastry, Heim, Belfield et al. (2024) | Foundational large-scope paper on compute as an AI-governance lever — 19 authors incl. Sastry (OpenAI), Heim (GovAI), Belfield (CFI), Hadfield (U Toronto/Vector). Four properties of AI compute (detectability, excludability, quantifiability, supply-chain concentration) support three governance capacities (visibility, allocation, enforcement); 12 illustrative mechanisms across those capacities; explicit risks + guardrails; compute-uranium analogy. Load-bearing intellectual parent for EO 14110 compute-reporting, EU AI Act 10²⁵ FLOP threshold, chip export controls, and downstream research incl. Rahman 2026 | `Sastry_Hadfield__2024--compute_governance_ai.pdf` |
| Ball (2025) | Proposal for hybrid public-private frontier-AI governance: government-authorized private regulatory bodies certify frontier developers who opt in; certified firms receive tort-liability safe harbor for third-party misuse harms; multi-body competition prevents race-to-bottom via a decertification-cascade mechanism (revoking a lax regulator's license retroactively removes safe harbor for *all* its clients at once). Rejects compute governance, use-based regulation, tort-alone, and international governance as insufficient for the transition period. Explicit state- or federal-implementable. Directly inspired by Hadfield & Clark's Regulatory Markets 2023 — noteworthy author cross-link with Sastry et al. 2024 (Hadfield on both) | `BallD__2025--private_governance_frontier_ai.pdf` |
| Reuel, Bucknall et al. (2025) | TMLR published version of "Open Problems in Technical AI Governance" (arXiv:2407.14981, v2 Apr 2025). 33-author agenda paper (Stanford/Oxford/MIT-CSAIL/GovAI/Mila/Cambridge/Cohere/Hugging Face; incl. Bengio, Pentland, Kochenderfer, Trager, Anderljung, Heim) defining **Technical AI Governance (TAIG)** as technical analysis and tools supporting effective governance (identify / inform / enhance). 2-D taxonomy — 6 **capacities** (Assessment, Access, Verification, Security, Operationalization, Ecosystem Monitoring) × 4 **targets** (Data, Compute, Models & Algorithms, Deployment) — enumerating ~40 open sub-problem areas with example research questions. Explicit "reader guide" table mapping problem areas to research background (ML theory, cybersecurity, cryptography, hardware, software, social science). Direct upstream / meta-catalogue for compute governance (Sastry), evaluations (METR, Zhou et al.), verification, watermarking, incident regimes (Ortega), and evaluator-shopping mechanisms (AAIF) | `Reuel_Trager__2024--open_problems_technical_ai_governance.pdf` |

### Anthropic (US-based frontier lab; FAA-style regulation + economic-policy proposals)

Two institutional-branded frameworks released 2026-06-11 as the concrete policy artifacts behind Amodei's "Policy on the AI Exponential" essay (see Independent position essays below). Anthropic committed $350M ($200M research fund + $150M workforce fellowship) alongside their release.

| Document | Description | File |
|----------|-------------|------|
| Anthropic (2026, AAIF) | "Advanced AI Framework" — 19pp two-part proposal: (Part 1) frontier-developer obligations covering scope thresholds (>10²⁵ FLOP trained AND [>$500M AI-revenue OR >$1B AI-R&D-spend]), four enumerated risks (biological weapons / offensive cyber / loss of control / automated R&D), safety-framework requirements, six-month risk reports, system cards, 15-day critical-incident reporting, independent evaluator regime (with anti-evaluator-shopping mixer), security program for model weights, whistleblower protections, tiered enforcement options; (Part 2) societal-resilience measures on biological (prevention/detection/preparedness layers, gene-synthesis screening, pathogen-agnostic biosurveillance) and cyber threats (open-source software funding, phishing-resistant identity, forward-deployed engineers, patch-at-scale) plus a shorter loss-of-control section. | Amodei (2024, MoLG) | "Machines of Loving Grace" (darioamodei.com, October 2024) — ~13,000-word aspirational-endstate essay; the "upside" companion Amodei explicitly cites at the opening of *The Adolescence of Technology* (2026) as its risks-side counterpart.
