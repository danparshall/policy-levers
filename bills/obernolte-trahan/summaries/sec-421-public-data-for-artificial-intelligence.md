# SEC. 421. PUBLIC DATA FOR ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SYSTEMS — summary

**One-line:** Directs the OSTP Director, through NSTC and the NAII Interagency Committee, to develop a prioritized list of federal datasets to curate and release for AI training and evaluation — a coordination exercise, not a release mandate.

## What it does

Inserts new § 5103A into Title LI of the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. 9411 et seq.). The OSTP Director, acting through the National Science and Technology Council and the NAII Interagency Committee and in consultation with the Advisory Committee on Data for Evidence Building (5 U.S.C. § 315), must (i) develop a list of federal-investment priorities for curating publicly available federal datasets useful for AI training/evaluation and (ii) identify a hosting location. Public comment is required before finalizing. A report to Senate Commerce and House Science is due one year after enactment. No dedicated appropriation is provided.

## Key provisions

- **Three prioritization criteria (§ 5103A(a)(2)(A)):** data advancing novel AI systems in the public interest; datasets from federally-funded scientific research; datasets unlikely to be created absent federal funding.
- **"Identify 20 datasets"** in the federally-funded-research bucket specifically (§ 5103A(a)(2)(B)) — the only hard number in the section.
- **Eleven considerations (§ 5103A(a)(3)(A)–(K))** including OSTP's societal/geostrategic challenge list under 42 U.S.C. § 19107, key technology focus areas, representativeness of the full U.S. population, national-security release risks, and alignment with both the 2016 NAI R&D Strategic Plan *and* the July 2025 "Winning the Race, America's AI Action Plan."
- **Interagency Committee shall ensure** consistency with federal privacy law (technology/privacy standards of the National Secure Data Service under 42 U.S.C. § 19085(f)) and **shall bar data sharing** with any country the Secretary of Commerce (with Defense, State, Energy, DNI) deems detrimental to national security (§ 5103A(b)(3)–(4)).
- **Agency-created datasets shall be added** to the agency's comprehensive data inventory under 44 U.S.C. § 3511(a); **may** be made available to NAIRR pilot and DOE programs under EO 14110 (§ 5103A(c)).
- **Rules of construction (§ 5103A(e)):** nothing requires disclosure of trade secrets, CBI, or privileged information; agency heads retain full authority to withhold from public datasets.

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** OSTP Director (lead), NSTC, NAII Interagency Committee, Advisory Committee on Data for Evidence Building. NSF, DOE, and Commerce carry named implementation roles.
- **Regulated parties:** No new obligations on private entities. Federally-funded researchers are named as a target of *future* incentive recommendations (§ 5103A(d)(6)) — not new duties.
- **Beneficiaries:** AI researchers and small/medium U.S. businesses in named sectors (agriculture, healthcare, transportation, manufacturing, biotech, communications, weather) needing curated federal data; NAIRR (§ 423) as downstream consumer.

## Cross-references

- **Depends on / paired with:** § 423 (NAIRR) — § 421 is the upstream supply-side, NAIRR is the demand-side platform. § 422 (Grand Challenges) shares the OSTP-through-NSTC coordination pattern.
- **External statutes:** NAII Act § 5103 (15 U.S.C. 9413); R&D Competition & Innovation Act § 10387 (42 U.S.C. 19107) for challenge list; § 10375(f) (42 U.S.C. 19085(f)) for privacy standards; 44 U.S.C. § 3511 (federal data inventory); 5 U.S.C. § 315 (Data for Evidence Building Advisory Committee); 5 U.S.C. § 403 (agency definition).
- **Executive actions:** EO 14110 (Biden, Oct 2023 — largely rescinded Jan 2025 but still cross-referenced here); "Winning the Race, America's AI Action Plan" (July 2025 Trump plan).

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **No release mandate.** § 5103A(e)(2) explicitly preserves agency discretion to withhold, and (b)(3) requires consistency with all federal privacy law without prioritization. The section produces a list; it does not force publication. Confidentiality regimes (FISMA, HIPAA, FERPA, IRS § 6103, Census Title 13/26) all remain intact and untouched.
- **No appropriation.** The section describes "Federal investment in creating or improving" datasets but authorizes no funds. Absent a companion appropriation, this is a within-existing-resources coordination task.
- **"20 datasets" is oddly specific and narrow.** The number applies only to the federally-funded-research bucket (§ 5103A(a)(2)(A)(ii)), not the "public interest" or "market failure" buckets. Unclear why the middle bucket gets a headcount and the others don't.
- **EO 14110 cross-reference is stale.** Trump rescinded EO 14110 in January 2025. The bill still references it for NAIRR/DOE availability rules (§ 5103A(c)(2)) — either an oversight or a bet that any successor EO will fill the reference slot.
- **Track record.** OMB's Federal Data Strategy (2019–2023) and Evidence Act (2018) implementation both produced years of listing and cataloging with limited actual release. Nothing in § 421 structurally distinguishes it from that pattern — no deadline for release (only for the list), no forcing mechanism, no default-open presumption.
- **"Publicly funded research data" plays into CHIPS+Science-era open-access norms** but the section doesn't align with or codify the OSTP August 2022 public-access memo (Nelson memo) requiring immediate open access to federally-funded research outputs. A cross-reference would strengthen the mechanism.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Legitimately useful for U.S. AI competitiveness if it produces real releases — training-data bottlenecks are real, and federal agencies hold uniquely valuable corpora (NOAA weather, CMS claims, USDA agriculture, Census). Supporters can point to the sector list (§ 5103A(a)(3)(C)) as evidence of concrete downstream impact.
- **National security:** § 5103A(b)(4) creates a Commerce-led adversary blacklist for downstream sharing — a modest export-control hook that pairs with GAAIA's broader adversary-tech-transfer framing.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Largely orthogonal. This is a capability-enabling section (more/better training data), not a safety section. No risk-tiering of datasets by dual-use potential beyond the generic national-security consideration in (a)(3)(F).
