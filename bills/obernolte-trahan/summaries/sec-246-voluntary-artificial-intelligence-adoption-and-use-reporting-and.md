# SEC. 246. VOLUNTARY AI ADOPTION AND USE REPORTING AND DATA-SHARING PARTNERSHIPS — summary

**One-line:** BLS-run voluntary program for AI developers and deployers to submit anonymized workforce-adoption data under CIPSEA + FOIA confidentiality and a statutory no-regulatory-enforcement / no-antitrust-use bar; DOL publishes aggregates every 6 months, reports to Congress at 2 years, funded at $7M over FY2026–2030.

## What it does

The Secretary of Labor, through the Commissioner of Labor Statistics and coordinating with Commerce and OSTP, "shall establish a program to enable and encourage" AI developers, deployers, and other private entities to share "on a voluntary basis, anonymized data on the adoption or use of artificial intelligence in the workforce" (§ 246(a)), via MOUs or data-licensing agreements (§ 246(b)). Submissions are walled off from FOIA and from regulatory or antitrust use, publishable only in aggregate.

## Key provisions

- **Statistical-purpose lock-in + FOIA shield (§ 246(c)(1)(A)–(B)):** "Exclusively for statistical purposes" (44 U.S.C. § 3561); CIPSEA-confidential; FOIA-exempt via 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(3).
- **No enforcement / no antitrust use (§ 246(c)(1)(D)):** Hard statutory safe harbor — the load-bearing carrot.
- **Methodology (§ 246(c)(2)):** Statistical validity "to the extent feasible," uniform definitions, selection-bias mitigation.
- **Opt-in public roster (§ 246(d)):** Only participants that separately consent are listed — firms can participate anonymously.
- **Publication (§ 246(e)):** Machine-readable aggregates ≥ every 6 months, unless the Secretary determines publication would be "more detrimental than beneficial" to the data-sharing goal.
- **Appropriation (§ 246(g)):** $7M for FY2026–2030 (~$1.4M/yr — BLS-pilot scale).
- **Section-specific definitions (§ 246(h)):** "AI developer" and "AI deployer" defined broadly with no size or compute gate — deliberately broader than § 101's frontier thresholds.

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None mandatorily. Eligible pool: any firm that trains, owns, or operates AI.
- **Empowered actors:** Commissioner of Labor Statistics; Commerce and OSTP (coordination); Chief Statistician and Workforce Information Advisory Council (aggregation consultation).
- **Beneficiaries:** Public and researchers via aggregate statistics only (no microdata channel); participating firms in exchange for CIPSEA + FOIA + no-enforcement/antitrust cover.

## Cross-references

- § 246(h) supplies its own developer/deployer definitions, *not* § 101's frontier-scoped ones.
- Statutory borrowings: 44 U.S.C. § 3561 and ch. 35 subch. III (CIPSEA); 5 U.S.C. § 552(b)(3).
- Paired with § 247 (mandatory federal-survey AI questions) and § 248 (standardized data elements). Contrast with § 111 (mandatory transparency for large frontier developers, § 101(15) >$500M revenue) — the two together create a bimodal disclosure regime.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Voluntary with no counterweight.** No contract-eligibility, procurement, or safe-harbor consequence attaches to non-participation. Absent one, the dataset will be self-selected — the value-destroying failure mode for adoption statistics. § 246(c)(2)(A)'s "to the extent feasible" is candid: no post-hoc weighting fully corrects a self-selected pool.
- **Definitional divergence from § 101** invites conflation of "AI developer" across subtitles.
- **No researcher microdata access** — no FSRDC-style channel; even other federal agencies have no statutory hook.
- **"More detrimental than beneficial" suppression (§ 246(e)(1))** has no procedural gate or judicial review.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** BLS is the right operator and CIPSEA the right frame, but voluntariness likely yields an unrepresentative adoption dataset. Workforce-composition data (less politically sensitive) may fare better than adoption data.
- **Bimodal disclosure structure.** § 111 mandates public disclosure from ~5 large frontier labs on model safety; § 246 leaves the vast middle (Fortune 500 buyers, mid-tier vendors, cloud deployers) on a voluntary CIPSEA-shielded track for workforce adoption. Coherent design choice — different problems — but worth naming as a choice, not an oversight.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Supporters will highlight the no-enforcement / no-antitrust bar and CIPSEA/FOIA shields as respecting proprietary information.
