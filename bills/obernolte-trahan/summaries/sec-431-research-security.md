# SEC. 431. RESEARCH SECURITY — summary

**One-line:** Conforms all GAAIA-authorized activity to two existing federal research-security regimes; creates no new obligation of its own.

## What it does

Rather than write fresh research-security rules for AI activities, § 431 points to statutes already on the books. It requires that "the activities authorized under this Act shall be carried out in accordance with" (1) Subtitle D of title VI of the Research and Development, Competition, and Innovation Act — the CHIPS-and-Science research-security subtitle (42 U.S.C. 19231 et seq.), which covers malign foreign talent recruitment bans, foreign-support disclosure, and agency research-integrity policies — and (2) Section 223 of the FY21 NDAA (42 U.S.C. 6605), the DOE / federal-research-agency foreign-talent-recruitment-program provisions.

## Key provisions

- Cross-reference to CHIPS/R&D research-security subtitle (§ 431(1))
- Cross-reference to NDAA FY21 § 223 (§ 431(2))
- No new penalty, agency, or process created inside § 431 itself

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** Any recipient of GAAIA funding, fellowship, or contract — testbeds, DOE labs, university PIs, AI Safety Institute grantees
- **Empowered actors:** Existing research-security offices at NSF, DOE, and other funding agencies (no new authority granted here)
- **Beneficiaries:** National-security posture; no direct private beneficiary

## Cross-references

- **Depends on / paired with:** § 432 (Certifications and Audits of Temporary Fellows) extends the research-security posture with a specific new certification — non-federal temporary fellows / contractors / consultants and the agency head must sign, pre-engagement, that the fellow will not perform inherently governmental functions.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- Because § 431 only conforms to existing law, its bite depends entirely on the enforcement vigor of the referenced regimes. If Congress weakens CHIPS Subtitle D or NDAA § 223 later, § 431 automatically weakens with them — the incorporation is by reference, not by copy.
- No AI-specific research-security concern (model weight security, dual-use eval sharing, red-team results handling) is addressed here. Anything AI-unique is left to agency discretion or to § 432's fellow-level certification.

## Policy conversation angles

- **National security:** The section is a "we're not silent on China" placeholder. Hawks will read it as adequate baseline; skeptics will note it adds no AI-specific export or weight-security control.
