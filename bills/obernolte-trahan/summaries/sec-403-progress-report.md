# SEC. 403. PROGRESS REPORT — summary

**One-line:** Requires a one-time report to Congress, due one year after enactment, on implementation of the Title IV Program established under § 401.

## What it does

Directs the Under Secretary of Commerce for Standards and Technology (i.e., the NIST Director) — coordinating with the Secretary of Commerce and the Secretary of Energy — to submit a single implementation report to Congress within one year of enactment. The report covers the Program stood up under § 401 and the agreements entered into under § 402(b). No follow-up cadence is specified; this is a one-shot progress check, not a recurring accountability mechanism.

## Key provisions

- **Deadline:** not later than one year after enactment (§ 403(a)).
- **Lead agency:** Under Secretary of Commerce for Standards and Technology, in coordination with the Secretaries of Commerce and Energy (§ 403(a)).
- **Recipient:** "Congress" — no committee of jurisdiction named (§ 403(a)).
- **Required contents (§ 403(b)):**
  1. Agreements, statements of work, project schedules, and deliverables for the § 401(a) Program.
  2. Total dollar amount of agreements entered into under § 402(b).
  3. "Any other information the Under Secretary determines appropriate."

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None directly — this is an internal reporting obligation on NIST/Commerce/Energy.
- **Empowered actors:** Under Secretary of Commerce for Standards and Technology (report author); Secretaries of Commerce and Energy (coordinating).
- **Beneficiaries:** Congressional oversight, nominally. In practice, whichever committees choose to pick up an untargeted "submit to Congress" report.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** None from § 101 are load-bearing here.
- **Depends on / paired with:** § 401 (establishes the Program being reported on) and § 402(b) (agreements whose dollar totals must be disclosed). Without § 401/§ 402 having content, § 403 has nothing to report on.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Missing from Trahan section-by-section.** § 403 does not appear in `gaaia_section_by_section.txt`. This is the opposite of the § 234 problem (in the summary but not the bill body); here the section exists in the bill but is not described in the office's summary. Direction of the drafting inconsistency: bill body has content the summary lacks.
- **"Sections 401" (plural noun, singular section).** § 403(a) reads "the implementation of sections 401" — grammatical artifact. Presumably intended to read either "section 401" or to cross-reference all of §§ 401–402. Worth clarifying which before markup.
- **No committees of jurisdiction named.** "Submit to Congress" without specifying House Science, Space, and Technology / Senate Commerce, Science, and Transportation (the natural NIST committees) is unusually loose drafting for a report requirement. Typical bills name the receiving committees so the report actually lands somewhere accountable.
- **One-shot, not recurring.** No annual or biennial follow-up. If the Program under § 401 runs for multiple years, Congress sees implementation data once and then goes dark.
- **"Any other information the Under Secretary determines appropriate" (§ 403(b)(3))** is broad — gives the agency full discretion over what else is included, which cuts both ways (flexibility vs. no floor on substance).

## Policy conversation angles

- **Oversight / accountability:** Weak as an oversight lever — one-time report, no committees named, no consequence for late or thin filing. If a Hill office wants real visibility into how the § 401 Program is running, § 403 would need amendment to (a) name committees of jurisdiction, (b) require recurring reports, and (c) tighten mandatory contents beyond "agreements and dollar totals."
