# SEC. 402. COORDINATION, REIMBURSEMENT, AND SAVINGS PROVISIONS — summary

**One-line:** Fiscal plumbing for § 401 — makes SecCommerce responsible for de-conflicting Subtitle A with DOE and industry, and defaults DOE/National Lab resources loaned to NSF/NIST to a paid, agreement-based footing with a Commerce-side waiver.

## What it does

Directs SecCommerce to "take such actions as may be necessary" to prevent duplication between Subtitle A (the § 401 testbed program) and DOE research entities or private-sector work (§ 402(a)). Any DOE resources — advanced computing, testbeds, expertise — provided to NSF, NIST, or "any other applicable entity" under Subtitle A must be furnished on a reimbursable basis and under a written agreement (§ 402(b)), with a SecCommerce waiver where "necessary or appropriate to carry out a mission of the Department of Commerce" (§ 402(c)). A savings clause preserves DOE authority under NAII Act § 5501 (15 U.S.C. 9461) and blocks Commerce/NSF from using DOE or National Lab monetary resources (§ 402(d)).

## Key provisions

- **Anti-duplication duty is bare** (§ 402(a)): no report, no certification, no stop-work authority, no DOE dispute mechanism.
- **Reimbursement default** for DOE compute, testbeds, expertise (§ 402(b)).
- **Waiver held by Commerce, not DOE** (§ 402(c)): Commerce approves non-payment; DOE, which owns the resource, does not.
- **Savings** (§ 402(d)): preserves 15 U.S.C. 9461; bars Commerce/NSF from tapping DOE funds.

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None directly.
- **Empowered actors:** SecCommerce (waiver, nominal anti-duplication trigger); DOE indirectly (retains § 5501 authority and Lab budget sovereignty).
- **Burdened parties:** NSF, NIST, and § 401 testbed participants routed through them — they must budget for full-cost DOE reimbursement absent waiver.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "National Laboratory," "advanced computing resources," "testbeds" (per § 101 / § 401 usage).
- **Depends on / paired with:** § 401. Without § 402, testbed cost basis is undefined; without § 401, nothing to reimburse.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Anti-duplication has no teeth.** § 402(a) grants no cross-agency authority; a DOE/Commerce dispute becomes an OSTP/OMB fight, not a statutory outcome.
- **Waiver asymmetry.** DOE forgoes revenue but does not hold the waiver; Commerce, which would pay, does. Commerce must defend any waiver as advancing a *Commerce* mission — defensible for NIST-led work, harder for NSF- or industry-led activity.
- **"Any other applicable entity" is undefined** — read broadly it covers private testbed participants routed through NSF/NIST; read narrowly, only federal entities.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** § 401 sells itself as a public–private front door, but "reimbursable basis" at DOE means full cost recovery — direct labor plus 40–60% overhead. Startups and academic groups the testbed most needs to reach cannot afford Lab hours at that rate. § 401's promise is only real if § 402(c) waivers are used liberally — a policy choice buried in a "procedural" section.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** The § 401 red-teaming and vulnerability-assessment capacity is safety-relevant only if outside parties actually use it. Cost-gated access means underutilized safety infrastructure.
- **National security:** DOE traditionally prefers full-cost programmatic funding over reimbursable sharing. § 402 constrains that preference, but only when Commerce will pay or waive. Expect DOE to lobby against liberal waivers.
