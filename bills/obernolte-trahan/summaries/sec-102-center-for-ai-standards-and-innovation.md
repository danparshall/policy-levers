# SEC. 102. CENTER FOR AI STANDARDS AND INNOVATION — summary

**One-line:** Establishes CAISI at Commerce as the central federal AI standards, evaluation, and IVO-licensing body — Secretary-appointed Director, above-GS hiring, $100M/yr FY27–29 plus fee authority, 3-year sunset.

## What it does

Amends the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 (Pub. L. 116-283 div. E) to insert a new SEC. 5304 creating CAISI at Commerce, plus statutory definitions of "AI interpretability" and "adversarial robustness" at 15 U.S.C. § 9401(4)–(5). The Secretary of Commerce stands CAISI up within 60 days of enactment (§ 5304(a)(1)) after consulting OSTP/DOE/DOD/DHS; the Director is Secretary-appointed within 3 months of that (§ 5304(b)). CAISI runs two workstreams: voluntary technical standards (adversarial robustness, interpretability, model tampering, supply chain, synthetic-content provenance, red/blue-teaming), and licensing/disciplining the IVOs that audit large frontier developers under §§ 111 and 112.

## Key provisions

- **27 statutory duties at § 5304(c)(1) (A–AA), almost all voluntary.** CAISI is NIST-style, not a rulemaker for technical standards. Operative core: (A) security standards with NIST/DOD/IC; (B) evaluations of U.S. and foreign-competitor AI; (E) reliability/interpretability/data-center/hardware standards; (P) CBRN + energy safeguards; (X) IVO administration; (Y)–(Z) research + advisory role.
- **IVO administration (§ 5304(c)(1)(X)) — CAISI's main regulatory tooth.** Licenses/relicenses IVOs, sets qualifications and COI rules, receives audit and assessment results under § 112, refers matters to AG under §§ 111–112, publishes redacted aggregate reports, and suspends/revokes licenses.
- **Confidentiality / anti-regulatory-use shield (§ 5304(d)).** Info voluntarily shared by "covered entities" is FOIA (b)(3) exempt AND may not be used by any Federal, State, local, or Tribal government to regulate the sharing entity.
- **Funding:** $100M/yr FY27–29 (§ 5304(e)), no inflation escalator, covers §§ 5304, 111, and 112, available until expended. Fee authority (§ 5304(f)) lets the Director set cost-recovery fees on licensed IVOs and large frontier developers via APA § 553 rulemaking, deposited in a new "Center for AI Standards and Innovation Fund" and available without further appropriation.
- **Above-GS hiring (§ 5304(j)(2)):** Secretary may hire scientific/engineering/professional staff notwithstanding 5 U.S.C. § 3104 or other classification/compensation laws, at rates up to 3 U.S.C. § 104 (VP salary, ~$285K). Separately, § 102(b) repeals § 6(c) of the NIST Act (15 U.S.C. 275) and its 15-person cap on NIST critical-technical hires.
- **Structural:** Director sits in any EOP AI interagency process, may submit directly to NSC/OSTP (§ 5304(c)(4)); annual report to House Science and Senate Commerce on budget, tech-stack trends, foreign competition, talent gaps, emerging capabilities (§ 5304(g)); section sunsets 3 years after enactment (§ 5304(i)).

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** Large frontier developers (§ 101(15): >$500M prior-year gross revenue including affiliates) pay CAISI fees and face IVO oversight; IVOs (§ 101(14)) sit under CAISI's licensing regime.
- **Empowered actors:** Secretary of Commerce, CAISI Director, NIST, DOJ (via § 5304(c)(1)(X)(iv) referrals); Director seated in EOP/NSC/OSTP AI processes.
- **Beneficiaries:** NIST (hiring cap lifted); federal agencies (evaluation resources under (N)); "covered entities" (§ 5304(h) — any private/nonprofit/consortium with "demonstrated ability to develop or evaluate advanced artificial intelligence systems"), which get the § 5304(d) shield.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms:** "large frontier developer" (§ 101(15)); "IVO" (§ 101(14)); "catastrophic risk mitigation" (§ 101(3)); "foreign adversary" defined in-line at § 5304(c)(3) via 10 U.S.C. § 4872(f)(2).
- **Depends on:** §§ 111 and 112 — CAISI's IVO-licensing role is only meaningful because those sections impose the underlying duties.

## Notable statutory language

> Information shared with the Center by a covered entity "may not … be used by any Federal, State, local, or Tribal government to regulate an activity of such entity with respect to which such information relates." (§ 5304(d)(2)(B))

Combined with the broad § 5304(h) covered-entity definition, this is preemption-adjacent: voluntarily-shared information cannot be repurposed by state AGs or other regulators against the entity that provided it.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **3-year sunset (§ 5304(i)) is structurally decisive.** CAISI, its IVO licensing authority, and its fee stream all terminate. If not reauthorized, IVO licenses are orphaned and the § 111/112 enforcement pipeline loses its administrator. The Trahan section-by-section does not flag the sunset.
- **§ 5304(d)(2)(B) scope is ambiguous.** "Regulate an activity" is not carved out for § 111/112 state-AG enforcement — a defendant could plausibly argue it forecloses derivative use of CAISI-channel facts by state AGs.
- **Pay ceiling reality-check.** 3 U.S.C. § 104 ≈ $285K/yr — real vs. GS-15 (~$195K), but well below senior frontier-lab compensation. With no inflation escalator on $100M/yr and cost-capped fees (§ 5304(f)(3)), CAISI cannot backfill funding through IVO revenues.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** IVO administration (§ 5304(c)(1)(X)) and CBRN safeguards (P) are real levers, but the voluntary character of most duties, the 3-year sunset, and § 5304(d) materially limit downstream teeth. AISI-to-CAISI transition advocates should scrutinize whether CAISI can independently publish company-specific findings given § 5304(d).
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Supporters will emphasize voluntary-standards framing, the § 5304(d)(2)(B) shield, and the hiring-cap lift as pro-industry design.
- **National security:** Duties (B), (D), (T), the § 5304(c)(3) foreign-adversary definition, and § 5304(c)(4) NSC coordination give this office a national-security tilt.
