# SEC. 232. COMMUNITY COLLEGE AND AREA CAREER AND TECHNICAL EDUCATION CENTERS OF AI EXCELLENCE — summary

**One-line:** Amends the NAII Act of 2020 to direct NSF to designate up to eight "Centers of AI Excellence" at community colleges and area CTE schools — with no dollar authorization, no sunset, no matching funds, and evaluation criteria left entirely to the NSF Director.

## What it does

Rewrites 15 U.S.C. 9451(e)(3)(B) to instruct the NSF Director — coordinating with Commerce's Regional Technology and Innovation Hub Program, and acting through NSF's Regional Innovation Engines, Advanced Technological Education (ATE) program, and other NSF programs — to designate not more than eight "regionally and geographically diverse" eligible applicants as Centers of AI Excellence (§ 232, amending § 5401(e)(3)(B)(i)). Centers must "enhance educational outcomes and drive workforce development by integrating [AI] into teaching, learning, and community engagement." Selected centers develop and disseminate best practices, scale successful programs, train educators, provide hands-on AI research opportunities, identify career pathways for students, and facilitate private-sector apprenticeships/internships/co-ops.

## Key provisions

- **Cap of 8 centers**, "regionally and geographically diverse" (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(i)). No floor.
- **Eligible applicant** = a community college or area CTE school (per Perkins Act § 3, 20 U.S.C. 2302) *in partnership with* at least one of: a Federal/State/local/Tribal/territorial government entity; an IHE; a private-sector entity; an economic or venture development organization; a labor or workforce training organization (which may include a state or local workforce development board) (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(vi)(II)).
- **Application must include** (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(ii)): focus areas aligned with private + federal regional investment; applicant capacity; current and anticipated workforce demand in related occupations; plan to support evaluation data collection; plan to use outcome-oriented measures.
- **Required activities** (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(iii)): disseminate best practices for AI research/education at community colleges and CTE schools; scale successful programs; equip teachers with AI-integration strategies; hands-on AI research opportunities; career-pathway identification; partnership facilitation with private-sector employers.
- **Partnerships** (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(iv)): NSF "shall encourage" applicants to partner with nonprofits or IHEs with extensive AI experience — permissive, not required.
- **Accountability** (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(v)): NSF evaluates against criteria "determined by the Director"; report to Congress and public not later than 180 days after evaluation completes, with results and any recommendations for administrative or legislative changes.

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None — grant/designation program, not regulatory.
- **Empowered actors:** NSF Director (full discretion over selection, activities delivery, evaluation criteria); Commerce Regional Tech & Innovation Hub Program (coordination role).
- **Beneficiaries:** Community colleges and area CTE schools, and their partners — private-sector entities, workforce boards, economic/venture development orgs, Tribal/State/local governments.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "area career and technical education school" (Perkins Act § 3, 20 U.S.C. 2302); "nonprofit organization" (35 U.S.C. 421); "venture development organization" (Stevenson-Wydler § 27, 15 U.S.C. 3722); "state/local workforce development board" (WIOA §§ 102, 107, 29 U.S.C. 3111, 3122). All external — no new definitions created except "eligible applicant" and "Center of AI Excellence" themselves.
- **Depends on / paired with:** Amends existing NAII Act structure (15 U.S.C. 9451). Sibling to § 211 (K–12 AI literacy) and § 233 (AI-in-education research with rural/low-income/Tribal emphasis). No cross-reference to NAIRR (§ 423) — designated centers get no explicit compute/data pipeline.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **No authorization of appropriations, no fiscal-year window, no per-center dollar cap.** The section adds no money; NSF would fund designations from existing ATE / Regional Innovation Engines lines. Given the "coordinate with" language, this may be intended as a *labeling* program that repackages existing awards rather than adding new capacity.
- **No sunset, no designation term.** The section-by-section is silent on whether a designation is permanent, renewable, or for a fixed period. The bill body is also silent. In practice this likely means designations continue at the Director's pleasure.
- **Evaluation criteria are entirely at Director's discretion** (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(v)(I)). The application must describe a plan to use "outcome-oriented measures" (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(ii)(V)), but "outcome-oriented" is undefined — job placement? wage gains? credential completion? enrollment? — and the Director sets the yardstick. Weak feedback loop for whether centers actually deliver workforce outcomes.
- **Report has no teeth.** The Director recommends "administrative or legislative action" (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(v)(II)(bb)) but there is no auto-deauthorization if outcomes are poor, no comparative benchmark against unfunded peers, no independent evaluator requirement.
- **Industry partners: obligatory but unencumbered.** A private-sector partner satisfies eligibility, but the section imposes no matching funds, no in-kind commitment, no governance/advisory role, and no data-sharing requirement. Industry gets association with a federally designated "Center of AI Excellence" and access to a student pipeline; it owes nothing back.
- **User vs. builder curriculum: ambiguous by design.** The activity list mixes "AI research and education" (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(iii)(I)) — which points at builders/implementers — with "career pathways in [AI]" and employer-partnership pipelines that at CC/CTE altitude historically train technicians and users. The section neither forces nor forbids either emphasis. Absent guidance, expect selected centers to gravitate toward whatever their private-sector partner already needs, which for most regional employers is AI *use* and low-code deployment, not model training.
- **"Regionally and geographically diverse"** (§ 5401(e)(3)(B)(i)) is the only equity anchor. No rural/low-income/Tribal preference (contrast § 233, which does have one). With only 8 slots and diversity as the sole criterion, expect one-per-region politics to dominate site selection.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** Nominally the strongest hook — CC/CTE schools are the front line for mid-career reskilling. But the mechanism is thin: 8 centers nationwide, no dollars, no matching, no defined outcome metric. If the ask is "help displaced workers transition into AI-adjacent jobs," this section is symbolic more than operational. Push interlocutors to specify what a Center of AI Excellence would actually *do* for a laid-off warehouse worker or paralegal, and how we'd know it worked.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Modest positive — a federal designation with NSF/Commerce coordination reduces duplication across states. But because designations layer on existing NSF programs rather than creating new capacity, don't oversell.
- **Bipartisan optics:** Community colleges and CTE schools are politically safe for both parties and appear in nearly every congressional district. The 8-slot cap + regional-diversity requirement is designed for exactly the kind of one-per-region distribution that generates broad House support. Read as a bipartisan-signaling section with a light-touch mechanism.
