# SEC. 201. AI LITERACY EFFORTS OF THE AI TASK FORCE — summary

**One-line:** Tells the NSF Director to "generally support" two existing NSF programs — the National STEM Teacher Corps pilot and Computer Science for All — in line with the December 2024 House AI Task Force report.

## What it does

Redirects existing NSF authority; does not create a new program, appropriate new money, or set a deadline. The Director "shall take such actions as may be necessary" to provide "general support" to the two named programs "in accordance with the recommendations of the AI Task Force established on February 20, 2024, of the House of Representatives." The referent is the Bipartisan House Task Force on Artificial Intelligence stood up by Speaker Johnson and Minority Leader Jeffries in Feb 2024, which released a ~270-page report in Dec 2024.

## Key provisions

- No dollar amount; no timeline; no reporting requirement (§ 201).
- Support scoped by reference to a non-statutory document (the Task Force report), not to any specific enumerated recommendation.
- Both target programs pre-date GAAIA: Computer Science for All is an existing NSF program (est. 2016); National STEM Teacher Corps is an NSF pilot authorized separately.

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** NSF Director (nominally — the "shall" has no enforcement hook).
- **Beneficiaries:** Two named NSF programs; downstream, K-12 teachers and students.

## Cross-references

- **Depends on / paired with:** Section-by-section (line 120) describes a § 234 "National STEM Teacher Corps" that "adds AI skills development" to the pilot and directs consideration of AI best practices for high school teachers. **§ 234 is not present in the bill body.** § 201 is the only place the Corps appears in the operative text, so § 201 is likely carrying whatever teeth § 234 was meant to have — and it does so with weaker language ("general support in accordance with recommendations" vs. a directive to add AI content).

## Drafting notes & open questions

- "In accordance with the recommendations" imports a ~270-page report by reference without specifying which recommendations bind NSF. An NSF Director could plausibly satisfy this with any nonzero effort on either program.
- The missing § 234 is the more substantive teacher-facing provision. Whether that omission is a drafting error or an intentional pull-back matters for anyone briefing on Title II's education agenda.
- Absent dollars or a report-back, this reads as a sense-of-Congress endorsement dressed as a directive.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Bipartisan cover — the "House AI Task Force" framing is the section's main political asset, not its mechanism.
