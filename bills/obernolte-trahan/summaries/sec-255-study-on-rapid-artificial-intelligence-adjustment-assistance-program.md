# SEC. 255. STUDY ON RAPID ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE ADJUSTMENT ASSISTANCE PROGRAM — summary

**One-line:** Directs Secretary of Labor to commission a design study — not a program — for a "Rapid AI Adjustment Assistance" analogue to Trade Adjustment Assistance (TAA), covering eligibility, benefits, cost, and interop with existing law.

## What it does

Requires the Secretary of Labor, "directly or by grant," to provide for a study on design options and challenges for a Rapid AI Adjustment Assistance Program to help workers dislocated or job-seekers impacted by AI (§ 255(a)(1)). The study must draw on data and forecasts developed elsewhere in the Act and on lessons from TAA under chapter 2 of title II of the Trade Act of 1974 (19 U.S.C. 2271 et seq.), and it must answer a specified menu of design questions (§ 255(a)(2)). Results must be published on a public DOL website within 2 years of enactment (§ 255(b)). Nothing here authorizes actual assistance to any worker.

## Key provisions

- **Timelines:** 12 months to "provide for" the study (§ 255(a)(1)); 24 months to complete and publish (§ 255(b)).
- **Vehicle:** Directly, or by grant to an outside performer (§ 255(a)(1)).
- **TAA as design anchor** and "other examples of adjustment assistance programs as appropriate" (§ 255(a)(2)(A)).
- **Eligibility design questions** include data valuable for determination, regulatory barriers to collecting/analyzing that data, evidence thresholds, whether DOL should use AI to adjudicate petitions, tiered/preliminary approval, and whether the process should be petition-based at individual, group, or "other" levels (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(i)(I)–(VI)).
- **Benefit design questions:** positive/negative effects of different forms of assistance, "including credits for retraining or education," plus transitions to employment "unlikely to face similar dislocation in the near future" (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(ii)).
- **Compatibility with existing laws and regulatory barriers** (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(iii)).
- **Evidence collection**, including pilot programs "with randomized assignment or other experimental methods where appropriate" (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(iv)) — an unusual and welcome ask.
- **Overall cost under different design choices** (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(v)).
- **Collaboration** among labor organizations, workforce development agencies, and educational institutions (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(vi)).
- **Appropriations:** $1,000,000 for the *period* FY2026–2030 combined, not annual (§ 255(c)).

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None directly — this is a study.
- **Empowered actors:** Secretary of Labor (author or grant-maker); DOL policy staff who would draft any successor legislation.
- **Beneficiaries:** No one directly. The intended eventual beneficiaries are AI-displaced workers — but only if Congress later enacts an actual program on the study's recommendations.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "Impacted worker" is defined only for this section (§ 255(a)(1)); no external § 101 term is invoked.
- **Depends on / paired with:** Draws on data/forecasts from Subtitle B (§§ 241, 243 Workforce Research Hub, 244 LMI pilot, 246 BLS voluntary reporting, 247 survey questions, 248 data elements) and § 252 (AI-sensitive occupation forecasts). Naturally pairs with § 251 (WARN Act AI-related layoff disclosures) as an upstream signal, and with § 257 (AI Workforce Policy Options Report) as a parallel policy-menu exercise.

## Notable statutory language

> "(1) IN GENERAL.—Not later than 12 months after the date of enactment of this Act, the Secretary shall, directly or by grant, provide for a study on potential design options and challenges for a Rapid AI Adjustment Assistance Program to help workers dislocated or job-seekers impacted by artificial intelligence …" (§ 255(a)(1))

The "provide for a study" phrasing is load-bearing: it triggers at commissioning, not delivery.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Section-by-section conflicts with the bill body on the 12-month deadline.** The section-by-section says "Within 12 months, the Secretary of Labor must *complete* a study." The bill text says the Secretary must "*provide for* a study" within 12 months, with completion and publication at 24 months (§ 255(b)). Cite the bill, not the summary.
- **"Rapid" is undefined.** The section asks the study to consider tiered/preliminary approval (§ 255(a)(2)(B)(i)(V)) but does not commit to anything faster than TAA's petition-based mechanism, which is the pace baseline it should improve on.
- **Attribution problem is not squarely asked.** The eligibility questions ask *how* to determine who qualifies as AI-impacted, but not the harder counterfactual: how to distinguish AI-caused displacement from cyclical unemployment, offshoring, broader tech adoption, or sectoral shifts. Without a stance on attribution, "rapid" adjudication may just mean rapid error.
- **Missing questions Canary should push to add:** benefit adequacy relative to wage loss (TAA's wage-differential benchmark is a natural starting point but is not named); moral hazard / employer incentive to relabel layoffs; adverse selection into the program; interaction with unemployment insurance, WARN, and WIOA (only "existing laws" is named generically at § 255(a)(2)(B)(iii)); sunset / phase-out design; portable-benefit or individual-account variants (the neighboring § 257 covers these, but § 255 does not cross-reference).
- **Funding is thin but plausible for scope.** $1M / 4 years for a design study — not a pilot — is on the low end but defensible if performed intramurally or via a single grant. It would be inadequate if the "pilot programs with randomized assignment" language in § 255(a)(2)(B)(iv) were meant to include actual pilots rather than a design for pilots.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** This is the strongest labor-facing hook in the bill. Adjustment assistance for AI-displaced workers is a headline demand from AFL-CIO, SEIU, and Writers Guild / SAG-AFTRA post-2023. A study-only vehicle can be attacked as delay, or defended as the necessary predicate to a real program — either framing is available to Canary depending on interlocutor. Suggested asks: explicit inclusion of benefit-adequacy analysis, wage-differential modeling, attribution methodology, and interaction with UI/WARN/WIOA.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** A federal AAA program pre-empts a scramble of state-level "AI severance" mandates. Framing to industry: predictable federal safety net is cheaper than 50-state variance.
- **State AG / enforcement:** N/A — pure study.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Indirect. The credibility of an AI-transition policy story to labor-aligned Members depends on visible workforce backstops; § 255 is the placeholder for that backstop but does not commit to one.
