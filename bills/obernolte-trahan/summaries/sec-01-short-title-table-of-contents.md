# SEC. 1. Short title; table of contents — summary

**One-line:** Names the Act the "Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026" and lays out its four-title structure via a full table of contents.

## What it does

Structural, non-substantive. § 1(a) fixes the short title. § 1(b) sets the TOC, which organizes the 269-page discussion draft into four titles: I — Frontier AI Governance (§§ 101–141, with subtitles on Definitions/CAISI, Transparency/IVOs/Whistleblowers, Federalization, Fraud, and Free Speech); II — Workforce (§§ 201–257, spanning AI education, labor-market data, and worker protections including WARN amendments); III — Cybersecurity (§§ 301–321, reauthorization plus open-source and model-weight security); IV — R&D and International Cooperation (§§ 401–432, testbeds, coalitions, NAIRR, and research security).

## Key provisions

- Short title: "Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026" (§ 1(a)).
- TOC governs the four-title, multi-subtitle structure (§ 1(b)).

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **§ 234 phantom.** The Trahan section-by-section (`gaaia_section_by_section.txt` lines 120–122) describes a "Sec. 234. National STEM Teacher Corps." No such section appears in the bill body, and — verified against the § 1(b) TOC — the TOC does not list it either. Subtitle II-A jumps from § 233 directly to § 241. Either the section was cut before circulation and the section-by-section wasn't updated, or a drafting-office omission cross-cuts both the TOC and body. Worth flagging to Obernolte/Trahan staff.
- **§ 403 asymmetry, reverse direction.** § 403 (Progress report) is present in the TOC and body but is absent from the Trahan section-by-section, which jumps 402 → 411. Minor, but confirms the section-by-section is not a reliable index of the body.
- **Numbering scheme.** Titles II–IV use tens-place subgrouping (201/211/221/231…, 301/311/321, 401/411/421/431), leaving room for insertions during markup. This is normal for a discussion draft and telegraphs where the drafters expect additions.
- **Placeholders remain.** The caption blocks show unfilled `To [________]` and a blank committee-of-referral line, consistent with pre-introduction status (no H.R. number yet).

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** The TOC surfaces Title I Subtitle C's federalization piece (§ 121) — the preemption hook supporters cite.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Title I Subtitles A–B (§§ 101–113) are where the CAISI, IVO, and transparency machinery sit; § 1 signals they lead the bill.
- **Worker / labor:** Title II is the longest title by section count (23 sections including the phantom § 234); its prominence is itself a talking point when the bill is framed as "just safety."
