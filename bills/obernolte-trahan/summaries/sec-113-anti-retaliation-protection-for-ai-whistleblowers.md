# SEC. 113. ANTI-RETALIATION PROTECTION FOR AI WHISTLEBLOWERS — summary

**One-line:** Creates a private federal whistleblower cause of action — modeled on SOX/AIR21 procedure — for employees and contractors who report suspected violations of federal AI law, with mandatory-arbitration waivers explicitly voided.

## What it does

Bars any employer from discharging, demoting, threatening, blacklisting, harassing, or otherwise discriminating against a "covered individual" for lawfully reporting an "AI violation" (or conduct they reasonably believe is one), or for testifying or assisting in a related investigation. Protected reports may run to regulators, the AG, law enforcement, Congress, or the individual's own supervisor chain (§ 113(a)(1)–(3)). Complaints go first to the Secretary of Labor under 49 U.S.C. § 42121(b) procedures (the AIR21/SOX burden-shifting regime); if DOL fails to issue a final decision within 180 days, the complainant may sue in federal district court with a jury-trial right (§ 113(b)(1)–(2)).

## Key provisions

- **Reasonable-belief standard** — no requirement that a violation actually be proven (§ 113(a)(1))
- **Remedy stack** (§ 113(b)(3)): reinstatement with prior seniority; **2× back pay with interest**; compensatory damages including litigation costs, expert-witness fees, and reasonable attorneys' fees; plus "any other appropriate remedy"
- **Statute of limitations** (§ 113(b)(2)(B)(ii)): 6 years from violation OR 3 years from discovery, whichever is longer — capped at 10 years absolute
- **Anti-waiver / anti-arbitration** (§ 113(c)): rights and remedies cannot be waived or altered by contract, including any pre-dispute arbitration agreement
- Also incorporates 5 U.S.C. § 2302(b)(8)–(9) protected-disclosure conduct by reference

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** *Any* "employer" engaged in commerce that pays a covered individual (§ 113(d)(6)) — the section is NOT limited to § 101 "large frontier developers." Combined with the very broad AI/"artificial system" definitions in § 113(d)(2)–(3), the shield reaches essentially any firm building, deploying, or integrating ML systems.
- **Empowered actors:** DOL/OSHA (initial administrative forum); federal district courts (after 180-day exhaustion); DOJ and state/federal regulators as recipients of protected disclosures
- **Beneficiaries:** Employees (current and former) and **independent contractors** (current and former) at AI-touching firms (§ 113(d)(5))

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "AI violation," "artificial intelligence," "artificial system," "covered individual," "employer" — all defined internally in § 113(d), not borrowed from § 101. Notable: § 113 declines to use the § 101 "frontier developer" gate.
- **Depends on / paired with:** § 111 (transparency framework — the substantive obligation whose violation is the paradigm case here) and § 112 (IVO audit findings — a plausible source of information a whistleblower might carry out).

## Notable statutory language

> "'AI violation' means any violation of Federal law, as well as any related rules and regulations, related to the development, deployment, or operation of artificial intelligence." (§ 113(d)(1))

The trigger is not confined to violations of *this bill*. Any federal statute or rule touching AI development/deployment (FTC Act, sectoral rules at NHTSA/FDA/EEOC, forthcoming CAISI regs) is inside the shield.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- The section-by-section says "Prohibits AI companies from…" — but the bill body defines no "AI company." The operative term is the generic "employer." This is broader-than-advertised, in a way that favors coverage.
- **Compensatory damages only** — no punitives, and no explicit "special damages" clause of the sort SOX contains. Modest weakness relative to Dodd-Frank § 922.
- 6-year/3-year/10-year SoL structure is materially more generous than SOX (180 days to DOL) and matches Dodd-Frank in spirit.
- Uses § 42121(b) rules and burdens — that means the plaintiff-friendly "contributing factor" causation standard, not McDonnell Douglas.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** This is the section that gives § 111 and § 112 informational teeth from inside firms. Without an inside line, an IVO working from documents the firm chose to hand over is a weaker check. CAIP and other AI-policy groups have flagged whistleblower shields as one of the highest-value low-cost provisions available — this section largely delivers on that ask: reasonable-belief standard, anti-arbitration, contractor coverage, and full fee-shifting are all real (not aspirational) teeth.
- **Worker / labor:** Independent-contractor coverage matters — many frontier labs use contractor red-teamers and data workers who would otherwise fall outside standard employee whistleblower regimes.
