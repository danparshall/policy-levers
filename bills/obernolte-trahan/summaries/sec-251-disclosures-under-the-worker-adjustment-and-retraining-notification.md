# SEC. 251. DISCLOSURES UNDER THE WORKER ADJUSTMENT AND RETRAINING NOTIFICATION ACT — summary

**One-line:** Amends the WARN Act to require employers filing a mass-layoff notice to disclose whether AI was a "substantial factor," describe the AI's type and use, estimate the share of job losses attributable to AI, and describe any pre-layoff upskilling — with a good-faith safe harbor and no new penalties.

## What it does

Adds a new subsection (e) to WARN Act § 3 (29 U.S.C. 2102). When an employer is already required to issue a 60-day mass-layoff notice under § 2102(a) *and* AI was a "substantial factor" in the layoff, the notice must contain four additional statements: (A) that AI contributed, (B) type and usage of the AI, (C) an estimated percentage of employment loss attributable to AI, and (D) any upskilling/retraining actions taken pre-layoff. The Secretary of Labor must issue guidance within 300 days on how employers make the "substantial factor" determination and estimate the percentage. Requirement applies to notices issued 1 year after enactment or later.

## Key provisions

- Four required disclosure elements — § 251, adding WARN Act § 3(e)(1)(A)–(D)
- Good-faith safe harbor: any good-faith statement (including the percentage estimate) satisfies the requirement — § 3(e)(2)
- DOL guidance deadline: 300 days after enactment — § 3(e)(3)
- Effective date: 1 year after enactment for any covered notice — § 3(e)(4)
- "Artificial intelligence" definition cross-refs "section 101 of such Act" — § 3(e)(5)

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** Only WARN-covered employers — businesses with 100+ employees issuing notices for a "plant closing" or "mass layoff" as defined in 29 U.S.C. 2101 (50+ workers laid off *and* ≥33% of the workforce at a single site, or 500+ workers regardless of share).
- **Empowered actors:** Secretary of Labor (guidance only; no new enforcement role).
- **Beneficiaries:** Workers at covered employers, and downstream users of the data (researchers, DOL, journalists) who would gain a first structured signal on AI-attributed job loss.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "Artificial intelligence" — § 101 of GAAIA (the bill body says "section 101 of such Act," which is ambiguous since the amended statute is the WARN Act; see drafting notes).
- **Depends on / paired with:** DOL "substantial factor" guidance under § 3(e)(3); loosely relates to § 255 (study of a Rapid AI Adjustment Assistance Program) but § 251 does *not* wire disclosure data into § 255 eligibility or into the § 252 forecasts.

## Notable statutory language

> "An employer satisfies the requirements under this subsection if the notice required under subsection (a) includes a good-faith statement with respect to the requirements under paragraph (1), including a good-faith estimate of the percentage required under subparagraph (C)…" — § 3(e)(2).

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **"Substantial factor" is undefined in statute** and punted to DOL guidance under § 3(e)(3). Term is borrowed from tort/employment causation doctrine but is not a WARN term of art — its content will be whatever DOL says by day 300.
- **Zero-percent estimate is compliant.** § 3(e)(2) accepts any good-faith estimate for subparagraph (C), including 0%. No audit trail, methodology disclosure, or attestation is required.
- **No new penalties.** § 251 adds a disclosure duty but leaves WARN's remedy structure untouched: back pay + benefits capped at the notice period (up to 60 days) plus a $500/day civil penalty to local government — enforced only by private suit (or by the local government for the civil penalty). DOL has no enforcement role under WARN.
- **"Such Act" ambiguity in § 3(e)(5).** The AI definition points to "section 101 of such Act." Grammatical antecedent is the WARN Act, which has no § 101 defining AI; intent is clearly GAAIA § 101. Should be corrected to reference GAAIA by name before markup.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** WARN disclosure is symbolic more than substantive. The base statute covers a narrow slice of job loss — 100+ employee firms crossing high headcount/percentage thresholds at a single site — while most AI displacement is expected to arrive as small-firm attrition, contractor non-renewal, and slowed hiring, none of which trigger WARN at all. Layered on top of a narrow trigger is a good-faith safe harbor with no methodology floor and no penalty for a self-serving 0% estimate. The provision generates a data signal (useful for researchers and journalists) but does not create leverage for affected workers. A stronger design would (i) require a methodology disclosure and third-party attestation above some employer-size threshold, (ii) create a floor penalty for materially inaccurate estimates, and (iii) explicitly feed § 251 disclosures into § 255 eligibility and § 252 forecasts so the data has downstream teeth.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Federal, uniform disclosure format preempts a plausible wave of divergent state AI-layoff notice laws (CA, NY, IL have all floated versions).
