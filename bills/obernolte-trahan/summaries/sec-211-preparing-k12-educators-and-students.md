# SEC. 211. PREPARING K–12 EDUCATORS AND STUDENTS FOR AN AI LITERATE FUTURE — summary

**One-line:** Discretionary NSF grant authority for universities and nonprofits to develop K–12 AI-literacy curriculum, educator PD, and educator-facing assessment tools — no dollar authorization, no match, no sunset.

## What it does

Authorizes ("may make awards") the NSF Director to fund research and development, on a merit-reviewed competitive basis, at institutions of higher education and nonprofits (or consortia) for K–12 AI-literacy work (§ 211(a)). Permitted activities span curriculum development, educator engagement, evaluation-tool development, professional development, hands-on learning tools, and curriculum augmentation (§ 211(b)(1)–(6)), plus a catch-all "any other activity the Director determines appropriate" (§ 211(b)(7)). The Director may run the program through new or existing NSF programs (§ 211(c)).

## Key provisions

- Awards are competitive and merit-reviewed (§ 211(a)); eligible recipients are IHEs, nonprofits, or consortia thereof.
- Curriculum funded must be "learner-centered," "project-based," and personalizable (§ 211(b)(1)(B)–(D)) — no other pedagogical constraints.
- Evaluation tools funded under this section are for "educators assessing proficiency in AI literacy" (§ 211(b)(3)) — student assessment, not program-outcome evaluation.
- PD activities may integrate "in-person, virtual, and distance learning experiences" for SEAs, LEAs, principals, educators, and other school leaders (§ 211(b)(4)).
- Director may deliver via "new or existing" NSF programs (§ 211(c)) — no requirement to stand up a distinct line.

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None — this is a grant program, not a regulatory section.
- **Empowered actors:** NSF Director (full discretion over awards, activities, delivery vehicle).
- **Beneficiaries:** IHEs, nonprofits, and (indirectly) SEAs / LEAs / K–12 educators.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "AI" borrowed from 15 U.S.C. 9401 (NAII Act of 2020) (§ 211(d)(1)); "institution of higher education" from HEA § 102 (§ 211(d)(3)); "K–12 education," "LEA," and "SEA" from ESEA § 8101 (§ 211(d)(4)–(5)); "nonprofit organization" from 35 U.S.C. 421 (§ 211(d)(6)). These are all standard cross-references — no new statutory definitions are created for education-side terms.
- **"AI literacy" (§ 211(d)(2))** is defined only in this section: "age-appropriate knowledge and ability to use AI effectively, critically interpret outputs, solve problems in an AI-enabled world, and safely and ethically utilize AI."
- **Depends on / paired with:** Related education provisions are § 232 (community-college "Centers of AI Excellence") and § 233 (NSF grants on AI teaching tools with rural/low-income/Tribal emphasis, plus a Rural and Low-Income Areas AI Collaborative). § 211 does *not* cross-reference NAIRR (§ 423) — no pipeline is established for K–12 curriculum work to draw on NAIRR compute/data resources.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **No authorization of appropriations.** The section as excerpted (lines 3148–3250 of the full text) contains no dollar amount and no fiscal-year window. Without an "authorization of appropriations" subsection, this is bare authority — appropriators would have to fund it against other NSF EHR priorities from scratch.
- **No matching / cost-share requirement,** no cap on award size, no minimum number of awards, no reporting requirement back to Congress. Compare to typical NSF EHR grant sections, which usually specify at least one of these.
- **"May" throughout.** § 211(a) says the Director "may" make awards; § 211(c) says the Director "may" use new or existing programs. Nothing compels NSF to stand this up at all.
- **"AI literacy" definition is thin.** Four verbs — use, interpret, solve, utilize — with an ethics adverb. No mention of technical concepts (what a model is, training data, statistical vs. deterministic behavior), no mention of civic/economic implications, no mention of AI's environmental cost, labor-displacement risk, or misinformation dynamics. A curriculum satisfying this definition could be almost purely tool-training ("here's how to prompt ChatGPT for a book report") and still qualify.
- **Evaluation tools ≠ program evaluation.** § 211(b)(3) funds tools "for educators assessing proficiency in AI literacy" — i.e. student-facing assessments. It does not fund evaluation of whether the funded curricula actually improve learning outcomes. Absent an outcomes-evaluation requirement, the section is likely to produce curriculum artifacts and PD workshops without evidence that either changes what K–12 students can do.
- **Catch-all in § 211(b)(7)** ("any other activity the Director determines appropriate") gives NSF broad flexibility but also makes the enumerated activities effectively non-binding.
- **No Title I interaction.** The section uses ESEA § 8101 terms for LEA/SEA but does not target Title I schools, low-income populations, or under-resourced districts. That equity work is offloaded to § 233. Cross-reference between §§ 211 and 233 would strengthen both.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Modest positive — federal funding for AI curriculum reduces the chance of 50 divergent state approaches. But because the section is discretionary and unfunded, don't overweight this.
- **Worker / labor:** Weak. The definition of "AI literacy" (§ 211(d)(2)) does not touch labor-market implications, economic disruption, or workforce transition. K–12 students trained under a § 211-funded curriculum would not necessarily learn anything about AI's effect on the jobs they're preparing for.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Nearly absent. "Safely and ethically utilize AI" in the definition (§ 211(d)(2)) is the only hook, and it points at personal safe-use, not systemic risk or model behavior.
- **Bipartisan optics:** This is the kind of section that scores easy bipartisan support (education, NSF, states) without committing to enforcement or dollars — useful for the bill's political posture, low-cost for either side. Read as a signaling section more than an operational one.
