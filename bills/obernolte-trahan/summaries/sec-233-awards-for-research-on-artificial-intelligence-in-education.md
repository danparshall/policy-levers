# SEC. 233. AWARDS FOR RESEARCH ON ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE IN EDUCATION — summary

**One-line:** Amends the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 to give NSF discretionary authority to fund research on AI teaching tools, teacher-prep models, and K–12 classroom integration for low-income, rural, and Tribal students, and to stand up a two-year peer-support "AI Collaborative" for rural and low-income areas.

## What it does

Adds two new subsections — (h) and (i) — to Section 5401 of the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. 9451). Subsection (h) lets the NSF Director "may make" competitive, merit-reviewed awards to IHEs, nonprofits, and consortia (including private-sector partners) to develop AI teaching tools/materials, prepare K–12 teachers to integrate AI, and produce professional-development models. Subsection (i) lets the Director "may carry out" — in the two years after enactment — a program building regional peer-support cohorts of educators, principals, and school leaders in rural and low-income areas.

## Key provisions

- Awards are competitive and merit-reviewed (§ 5401(h)(1)(A)), issued through new or existing NSF programs (§ 5401(h)(1)(B)).
- Eligible entities: IHEs, nonprofits, or consortia of IHEs/nonprofits with private-sector partners (§ 5401(h)(5)(A)).
- Recipients "shall" emphasize preparing incoming K–12 teachers to integrate AI and support research in instructional materials, teacher prep models, scalable professional development, and inclusion-oriented tools (§ 5401(h)(3)).
- Application "may include" descriptions of student demographics, regional partnerships, ethical concerns, and consultation with educators/industry/civil society (§ 5401(h)(2)(B)) — all optional.
- Rural and Low-Income Areas AI Collaborative operates through new or existing programs, "including the pilot program under section 10512(a)(2)(B) of the Research and Development, Competition, and Innovation Act (42 U.S.C. 19172(a)(2)(B))" (§ 5401(i)(2)).

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** NSF Director (discretionary authority throughout).
- **Beneficiaries:** IHEs, nonprofits, K–12 teachers, students in low-income/rural/Tribal populations.

## Cross-references

- **Amends:** § 5401 of the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. 9451), as further amended by GAAIA § 221.
- **Piggybacks on:** § 10512(a)(2)(B) of the R&D, Competition, and Innovation Act (42 U.S.C. 19172(a)(2)(B)) — the CHIPS+Science-era NSF regional-innovation pilot.
- **Defined terms:** "nonprofit organization" borrowed from 35 U.S.C. § 421 (§ 5401(h)(5)(B)).

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **No dollar authorization.** The section creates authority to make awards but appropriates nothing and sets no ceiling. Funding comes out of NSF's existing envelope unless a companion appropriations bill lands.
- **No matching requirement.** No cost share, no in-kind requirement, no state buy-in gate.
- **Discretionary throughout.** Both subsections read "may" — NSF can decline to run either program with zero statutory consequence.
- **Tribal students named, BIE absent.** § 5401(h)(1)(A) lists Tribal populations as an intended beneficiary group but the section requires no coordination with the Bureau of Indian Education, no consultation with Tribal governments, and no set-aside. Tribal inclusion here is aspirational language, not mechanism.
- **Ethics review is optional.** § 5401(h)(2)(B)(iii) — "potential ethical concerns and implications of teacher, faculty, and student interaction with artificial intelligence" — is a "may include" in the application, not a scoring criterion or requirement.
- **Collaborative has no dedicated structure.** § 5401(i) creates a program label ("Rural and Low-Income Areas AI Collaborative") but no governance body, no host institution, and no dedicated funding — NSF is just told it may run peer-support cohorts, possibly through an existing pilot.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Supply-side subsidy without outcome measurement.** The operative language is about developing tools, materials, and teacher training — not about evaluating whether AI in classrooms actually improves student outcomes. § 5401(h)(3)(B)(iv) references "tools for teaching and learning that lead to student success," but "lead to student success" is asserted as a property of the deliverable, not a measurement the grant funds. No RCT requirement, no learning-outcomes reporting, no evaluation set-aside. This is the same pattern as most ed-tech subsidy programs: the money moves through developers and teacher-prep programs, and whether kids learn more is left as an exercise for the reader.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Supporters will frame this as capacity-building for underserved schools and a light-touch federal role (NSF grants, not mandates).
- **Worker / labor:** Teacher-prep angle intersects with STEM workforce concerns (see § 234 on the National STEM Teacher Corps, which shares this subtitle).
