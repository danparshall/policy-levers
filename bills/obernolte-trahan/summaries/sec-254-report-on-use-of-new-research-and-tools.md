# SEC. 254. REPORT ON USE OF NEW RESEARCH AND TOOLS IN RECIPIENT SELECTION AND PROGRAM MEASUREMENT — summary

**One-line:** Within 2 years of enactment, the Secretary of Labor must deliver Congress a report describing how AI-related data, benchmarks, and forecasts developed elsewhere in the Act "will be incorporated" into WIOA, apprenticeship, and other Labor grant selection and performance-measurement criteria — a scoping report, not a use mandate, funded at $500K over FY26–FY30.

## What it does

Under § 254(a), the Secretary of Labor produces a single report, within 24 months of enactment, that (1) describes how the data, benchmarks, and forecasts "developed or collected pursuant to this Act *will be* incorporated" into selection or performance-measurement criteria for three grant families — WIOA Title I formula and competitive grants (29 U.S.C. 3111 et seq.), apprenticeship/pre-apprenticeship/training grants administered by Labor, and "other grant programs administered by and determined to be appropriate by the Secretary" (§ 254(a)(1)(A)–(C)); (2) identifies statutory or regulatory barriers to that incorporation; (3) describes how the Secretary will support states, localities, and applicants in using the tools to improve their applications and performance measures; and (4) recommends timelines, responsible offices, and resource needs. Consultation with the Workforce Information Advisory Council is required, along with consideration of input from Subtitle B activities (§ 254(b)). The report goes to Senate HELP and House Education & Workforce (§ 254(c)) and must be published in a machine-readable, publicly accessible format on a Labor website within 30 days of submission (§ 254(d)).

## Key provisions

- 2-year deadline for the report; single deliverable, no updates required (§ 254(a)).
- Scope of covered grant streams: WIOA Title I formula and competitive; apprenticeship, pre-apprenticeship, and training grants at Labor; other Labor grants "determined to be appropriate by the Secretary" (§ 254(a)(1)(A)–(C)).
- Report must identify statutory/regulatory *barriers* to using the tools — but is not required to propose removals (§ 254(a)(2)).
- Applicant-support description (§ 254(a)(3)) covers technical assistance to states, localities, and other WIOA applicants.
- Consultation with Workforce Information Advisory Council mandatory (§ 254(b)).
- Machine-readable public posting within 30 days of Congressional submission (§ 254(d)).
- **Authorization: $500,000 total for FY2026–FY2030** — ~$100K/year, a report-writing line (§ 254(e)).

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** Secretary of Labor (report author, and — via § 254(a)(1)(C) — the actor who chooses which additional grant streams are in scope); Workforce Information Advisory Council (consultation).
- **Beneficiaries:** Congressional committees of jurisdiction get a 2-year checkpoint on whether the Subtitle B research investments are actually reaching grant decision-making; states/localities/applicants get whatever technical-assistance framework the Secretary describes.
- **Regulated parties:** None directly. WIOA grantees are not required to do anything under § 254; the section binds only the Secretary to write a report.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "Secretary" — Subtitle B context implies Secretary of Labor; confirm against § 101 / subtitle-level definitions.
- **Depends on / paired with:** § 244 (AI-sensitive occupations, labor market data modernization — the "data" input), § 245 (NIST-led benchmarks for AI automation/augmentation — the "benchmarks" input), § 252 (AI-sensitive occupation employment forecasts — the "forecasts" input), § 253 (forecasting competition — additional forecast output), § 248 (workforce data standards — schema layer beneath all of the above), § 256 (parallel "consider" mechanism at the state WIOA-board level for in-demand occupation lists), Subtitle B of Title II generally (feeding consultation input under § 254(b)).

## Notable statutory language

> "describes how the data, benchmarks, and forecasts developed or collected pursuant to this Act *will be* incorporated into the selection or performance measurement criteria of — (A) programs of formula and competitive grants under title I of the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act …; (B) apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship programs, and training programs involving grants, administered by the Secretary; and (C) other grant programs administered by and determined to be appropriate by the Secretary" (§ 254(a)(1))

The future-tense "will be" is doing all the work. It presupposes incorporation without requiring it; a Secretary who concludes the tools are not yet fit for grant-selection purposes can satisfy this paragraph by describing a "will be incorporated when [conditions]" plan that never fires.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **This is a report, not a use mandate — and it is the load-bearing question for all of Subtitle B.** § 245 builds benchmarks, § 252 builds forecasts, § 244 modernizes data, § 248 standardizes schema. None of them attach downstream policy consequences. § 254 is the section where "do these tools ever drive a federal decision?" gets its answer — and the answer is "the Secretary will tell you in two years." A report produced by whichever Secretary of Labor holds office 24 months post-enactment is a very weak forcing function; there is no requirement that the report commit to actual incorporation, no follow-on deadline for rulemaking, and no default rule if the report is late or missing.
- **"Will be incorporated" preserves full discretion to say no.** The verb tense presumes incorporation, but § 254(a)(1) does not require it. A report that reads "the Department has determined that the tools developed under this Act are not yet sufficiently mature for use in grant selection or performance measurement; incorporation will occur when validated" would satisfy the section as drafted. Compare § 256, which at least requires states to "consider" AI-related forecasts when updating in-demand occupation lists — a lower bar than "use," but a nonzero one, and one that binds someone other than the Secretary who wrote the report.
- **Barriers paragraph has no removal requirement.** § 254(a)(2) requires identifying statutory or regulatory barriers to using the tools — a useful diagnostic — but does not require the Secretary to propose fixes, and Congress is under no companion obligation to legislate against the barriers identified. This makes the barriers section a candid catalog at best, an alibi for non-incorporation at worst.
- **WIOA performance metrics are already well-established.** WIOA Title I performance measurement runs on statutory indicators (employment rate 2nd/4th quarter after exit, median earnings, credential attainment, measurable skill gains, effectiveness in serving employers — 29 U.S.C. 3141). Layering AI-forecast-driven criteria on top would require either statutory amendment or negotiated adjustment factors — which is part of what § 254(a)(2) is meant to surface. The section does not resolve which mechanism the Secretary would use.
- **Scope creep is at Secretary's option.** § 254(a)(1)(C) — "other grant programs administered by and determined to be appropriate by the Secretary" — is unbounded on the upside (Labor could include ETA discretionary grants, Job Corps, etc.) and on the downside (Labor could report only on the two named categories). Neither Congress nor the applicants have a say in which grant programs get covered.
- **$500K over 5 years is a report-writing budget, not an implementation budget.** ~$100K/year covers a small team of analysts and consultation logistics. There is no appropriation for actually building the technical assistance framework that § 254(a)(3) requires the report to describe.
- **30-day machine-readable publication requirement (§ 254(d))** is welcome — a real transparency lever if the report ever produces incorporation commitments, but empty if the report is a "will be, someday" placeholder.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** This is where the Subtitle B research investment either lands or doesn't. Canary should track (a) whether the report, when delivered, names specific grant competitions and specific benchmark/forecast inputs to their scoring rubrics — or stays in "the Department is exploring" language; (b) whether the barriers paragraph produces a concrete legislative ask that could be attached to WIOA reauthorization; (c) whether the "other grant programs" catch-all is used to expand or contract scope. If none of those happen, the Subtitle B research is stranded — benchmarks and forecasts get produced but never wired to a federal decision. Push on this in Hill conversations: "What is the mechanism by which § 245 benchmarks actually change a grant award?" The honest answer under GAAIA as drafted is "there isn't one — § 254 asks the Secretary to tell Congress whether there will be one, in two years."
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Supporters can frame § 254 as the deliberate, sequenced approach — build the tools first (§§ 244, 245, 252), study incorporation before mandating it (§ 254), then legislate specifics if warranted. That framing is defensible for a two-year window but becomes stalling if no follow-on action is scheduled. The bill contains no automatic trigger for follow-on legislation.
