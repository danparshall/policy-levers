# SEC. 231. SCHOLARSHIPS AND FELLOWSHIPS IN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE — summary

**One-line:** Amends the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 to add permissive NSF authority for undergraduate/graduate AI scholarships, professional-development fellowships, and a nationwide outreach campaign — no appropriation, no service payback, no safety scoping.

## What it does

Amends 15 U.S.C. § 9451(e)(2) — striking "FACULTY" from the paragraph heading to broaden it — and appends five new subparagraphs (D)–(H). NSF "may" support scholarships and fellowships for undergraduate and graduate students at institutions of higher education, "including community colleges," in programs related to the "design, research, assessment, development, deployment, integration, or application" of AI (§ 231, adding § 9451(e)(2)(D)(i)). Separately authorizes AI Professional Development Fellowships (E) for students, faculty, incoming industry "professionals," and K-12 school professionals; a nationwide outreach campaign (F); baseline eligibility (G); and a 7-year retrospective report (H).

## Key provisions

- **Award structure (§ 9451(e)(2)(D)(iii)):** Tuition + fees + stipend + professional-development funds for up to 5 years; paid directly to the institution.
- **No appropriation.** The section is authority-only; no dollar figure and no separate authorization line. Runs on NSF's existing appropriations.
- **No service payback.** Unlike NSF's SFS (CyberCorps) cybersecurity scholarships, § 231 imposes no post-degree federal-service commitment. Eligibility (§ 9451(e)(2)(G)) requires only citizenship/national/LPR status, a demonstrated "commitment to a career in advancing the field of artificial intelligence," and acceptance of terms.
- **Discretionary priority areas (§ 9451(e)(2)(D)(ii)):** AI teaching at K-12/CTE/IHE, AI + advanced manufacturing, AI + agriculture. **No safety, alignment, evaluation, or catastrophic-risk priority listed.**
- **Broadening-participation outreach (§ 9451(e)(2)(D)(iv), (F)(ii)):** NSF "shall" conduct outreach to rural IHEs, rural-serving IHEs, Tribal Colleges and Universities, and EPSCoR jurisdictions; nationwide outreach campaign "may" prioritize underserved and rural areas.
- **Professional Development Fellowships (E):** Includes short residencies (180 days–1 year) for professionals to teach students, and K-12 teacher/counselor fellowships done in "partnership with private sector entities" — the private-sector partnership is required, not optional, for the (E)(ii) and (E)(iv) tracks.
- **Report (H):** NSF submits to Congress within 7 years; assessment "may" include completion rates and workforce entry by degree tier.

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** NSF Director (broad discretion — "may" throughout the operative clauses).
- **Beneficiaries:** Undergraduate + graduate AI students at 4-year IHEs and community colleges; K-12/CTE teachers; mid-career professionals moving into or out of academic AI teaching; TCUs, rural-serving IHEs, and EPSCoR-state institutions.

## Cross-references

- **Paired with § 232:** § 232 stands up up-to-8 community-college "Centers of AI Excellence." § 231 is **not** four-year-focused — community colleges are explicitly included in both (D)(i) scholarships and (E)(ii) professional-development supplementals — so the two sections overlap on the CC track rather than dividing four-year vs. two-year.
- **Underlying statute:** 15 U.S.C. § 9451(e) (National AI Initiative Act of 2020). Pre-amendment paragraph was faculty-scoped; the heading strike broadens it to cover students.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Permissive throughout.** Every operative verb in (D), (E), (F) is "may." The only "shall" attaches to rural/TCU/EPSCoR outreach (D)(iv) and the 7-year report (H)(i).
- **No safety scoping.** The subject-matter list is developer-track ("design, research, ... development, deployment, integration, or application") with no mention of assessment-for-safety, alignment, red-teaming, or evaluation as priority areas — a notable omission given § 102's CAISI standards agenda.
- **No payback mechanism** distinguishes § 231 from SFS. Advocates for a workforce-pipeline-into-government track (e.g., staffing CAISI, agency AI leads) would need to add a service-commitment clause; nothing in (G) supports it as drafted.
- **Report clock (7 years) is long** relative to the field's tempo and to CAISI's 3-year sunset (§ 5304(i)) — the first § 231 retrospective would arrive after CAISI has already terminated absent reauthorization.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** The absence of any safety/evaluation priority in the (D)(ii) list is the section's biggest miss for a Canary audience. A one-line addition ("AI safety, evaluation, and alignment") in (D)(ii) would cost nothing and route NSF discretion toward the workforce CAISI and IVOs will need.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Broad, non-preemptive, permissive workforce authority — an easy bipartisan "yes" vote with limited downside for industry.
- **Worker / labor:** Community-college inclusion + rural/TCU/EPSCoR outreach + K-12 teacher fellowships are the labor-facing hooks; the private-sector-partnership requirement on (E)(ii) and (E)(iv) is worth surfacing to labor stakeholders.
