# SEC. 247. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE QUESTIONS IN FEDERAL SURVEYS — summary

**One-line:** Within one year of enactment, Census and BLS must revise five named federal surveys to add or improve AI-use questions — with no new money and broad agency discretion over what "AI" means.

## What it does

Directs the Secretary of Commerce (through Census) and the Secretary of Labor (through BLS), in consultation with the Chief Statistician of the United States, to revise five specified surveys to "incorporate or improve questions relating to the adoption and use of artificial intelligence" (§ 247(a)). Content is prescribed at the topic level, not the item level — agencies design the actual questions. Either Secretary may narrow the operative scope of "AI" for their questions, including limiting it to language models or frontier systems (§ 247(d)(1)).

## Key provisions

- **Timeline:** 1 year from enactment (§ 247(a)).
- **Surveys — Census (§ 247(b)(1)):** Annual Business Survey; Current Population Survey; Business Trends and Outlook Survey.
- **Surveys — BLS (§ 247(b)(2)):** Occupational Requirements Survey; American Time Use Survey.
- **Required content (§ 247(c)(2)):** types of AI (must "enable important analytical distinctions... such as between language models and simple predictive models"); occupations/tasks most impacted; skill and training changes, including AI-development skill shortages; intensity and frequency of use; work outcomes.
- **Scope-narrowing authority (§ 247(d)(1)):** either Secretary may restrict "AI" — explicitly may limit to language models or exclude non-frontier AI.
- **Inter-agency consistency (§ 247(d)(2)):** Commerce and Labor "shall consult... as appropriate and feasible, facilitate consistency" — soft, not a harmonization mandate.
- **No new money (§ 247(e)):** "No additional amounts are authorized to be appropriated."

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None directly. Survey respondents (households for CPS/ATUS; establishments for ABS/BTOS/ORS) will see new items.
- **Empowered actors:** Census, BLS, Chief Statistician (consultation only).
- **Beneficiaries:** Labor economists, researchers, and Congress — anyone reasoning about AI's labor-market effects from currently fragmented private surveys.

## Cross-references

- **§ 241** (Information Collection) — public-comment and workshop output "shall, to the extent practicable," inform question design (§ 247(c)(1)). But § 247(d)(1) opens with "Notwithstanding section 241," letting agencies narrow AI scope regardless of § 241 stakeholder input.
- **§ 244** (Job-to-Job Flows) and **§ 246** (Voluntary AI Adoption Reporting) — § 247 is the population-survey leg of a three-part labor-data architecture: administrative flows, industry voluntary reporting, and household/establishment surveys.
- **§ 248** (Data Elements) — Labor's standardized workforce data elements are the natural harmonization backbone for § 247(d)(2), but § 247 doesn't require alignment.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Section-by-section undercount.** Trahan's summary names four surveys; the bill body names **five**. The Business Trends and Outlook Survey is dropped from the summary — despite being the highest-frequency of the five (biweekly) and the survey Census has actually used for AI-adoption tracking since 2023.
- **No appropriations = displacement.** Adding a CPS supplement or ATUS module runs into millions per survey (PRA clearance, cognitive testing, respondent burden, processing). § 247(e) forecloses new money, so new AI items must displace existing items. Expect agencies to prioritize the cheapest surveys (BTOS, ABS) and defer the expensive ones (CPS), which inverts the analytical value ordering — CPS is where researchers most want AI data.
- **OMB / PRA silence.** No exemption from OMB's 60+30-day PRA cycle or Statistical Policy Directive No. 1 content review. A one-year deadline is tight against a normal 9–18 month clearance cycle; agencies will miss the deadline or ship minimally-tested items.
- **Question underspecification, partly mitigated.** "Do you use AI?" gets ~100% "yes" from anyone whose email autocompletes. The bill helps by requiring type-distinctions (§ 247(c)(2)(A)) and allowing scope-narrowing to frontier/language models (§ 247(d)(1)). But nothing prevents compliance via a single yes/no plus coarse checkbox. Teeth are in agency design, not statute.
- **Consultation ≠ harmonization.** Census and BLS could ship materially different AI definitions and comply with § 247(d)(2).

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** Population-scale measurement leg of GAAIA's labor-data build-out (§§ 244, 246, 252). Load-bearing on paper, under-resourced in practice.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Federal-standardized AI-use data reduces reliance on private surveys (McKinsey, Stanford AI Index) with opaque methodology and tech-forward panels. Neutral across the aisle.
