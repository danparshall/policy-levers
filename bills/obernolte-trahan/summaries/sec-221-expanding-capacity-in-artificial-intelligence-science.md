# SEC. 221. EXPANDING CAPACITY IN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SCIENCE — summary

**One-line:** Amends the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 to direct NSF to run a competitive, merit-reviewed grant program for institutions of higher education that are *not* among the top 100 federal-R&D recipients — to broaden participation in AI research, education, and workforce development.

## What it does

Inserts a new subsection (g) into Section 5401 of the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 (15 U.S.C. 9451) (§ 221(2)). NSF's Director must stand up a program making competitive, merit-reviewed awards to eligible institutions of higher education (IHEs) or nonprofits, or consortia of either, for AI research capacity-building and partnerships (§ 221 → new § 5401(g)(1)). "Eligible" is defined mechanically: any IHE that, per NCSES data, is not on average among the top 100 institutions in federal R&D expenditures over the three-year period immediately before the award year (new § 5401(g)(2)). The section is authorization-only — it creates the program but appropriates no dollars and sets no matching requirement.

## Key provisions

- **Non-top-100 gate (new § 5401(g)(2)):** rolling three-year average of federal R&D expenditures, per National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics data. In practice this is the HERD (Higher Education R&D) survey NCSES publishes annually.
- **Consortium partners allowed (new § 5401(g)(3)):** IHE, nonprofit, executive agency, State/local/Tribal/territorial government, private-sector entity, or an AI research institute under § 5201 of the 2020 Act.
- **Use of funds — pick at least one (new § 5401(g)(4)):** (A) build/expand AI research programs; (B) faculty recruitment and professional development; (C) post-baccalaureate → grad-school pipeline programs; (D) provide or *broker* access to compute, networking, data facilities, and software-engineering support; (E) facilitate private–federal–academic collaboration; (F) workforce-broadening workshops; (G) integrate ethical and responsible practices into curricula; (H) NSF-discretionary "any other activity."
- **Outreach mandate (new § 5401(g)(5)):** NSF *shall* solicit applications from eligible IHEs and nonprofits and engage applicants "from all regions … especially … from underserved communities."
- **Non-duplication (new § 5401(g)(6)):** NSF must ensure this program is complementary to, not duplicative of, other federal programs.
- **"May consider" factors (new § 5401(g)(7)):** support for first-generation undergraduates, geographic diversity, and applicant resource constraints — permissive, not required.

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** none — this is a grant program, not a regulation.
- **Empowered actors:** NSF Director (with consultation authority over other agency heads the Director deems appropriate).
- **Beneficiaries:** IHEs and nonprofits outside the top 100 federal-R&D recipients. Politically the frame is HBCUs, HSIs, Tribal Colleges, and other MSIs, but the statutory gate is purely R&D-expenditure-based — the actual eligible pool is far broader (see below).

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "Executive agency" per 5 U.S.C. § 403 (new § 5401(g)(8)(A)); "nonprofit organization" per 26 U.S.C. § 501(c)(3) (new § 5401(g)(8)(B)). Does not use § 101 GAAIA definitions — this section is grafted into the 2020 NAIIA and uses its terminology.
- **Depends on / paired with:** cross-references AI research institutes established under § 5201 of the 2020 Act as permissible consortium partners. Sits alongside § 211 (K–12 AI literacy grants), § 231 (scholarships/fellowships), § 232 (community college Centers of AI Excellence) as the Title II education/workforce build-out.

## Notable statutory language

> "[A]n institution of higher education that, according to the data published by the National Center for Science and Engineering Statistics, is not, on average, among the top 100 institutions in Federal research and development expenditures during the three-year period immediately before the year of the award at issue." (new § 5401(g)(2))

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **No dollar authorization.** § 221 creates the program but names no funding level and sets no sunset. Actual funding depends on annual NSF appropriations — a program without an authorization number can end up as a line item that never materializes.
- **"Not top 100" is a very wide net.** NCSES's HERD survey ranks ~900 R&D-performing IHEs. Excluding only the top 100 leaves roughly 800 institutions eligible — including many well-resourced R2 state schools and private universities that are neither MSIs nor traditionally under-resourced. If the political intent is to target HBCUs, HSIs, Tribal Colleges, and other MSIs, the statutory mechanism does not do that; MSI status is nowhere in the eligibility test. It appears only glancingly via "underserved communities" outreach (new § 5401(g)(5)(B)) and "resource constraints" as a permissive consideration (new § 5401(g)(7)(C)).
- **No matching requirement, no sustainability test.** The bill does not require applicants to demonstrate they can sustain the program past the grant period or bring cost-share. For capacity-building at institutions with weak indirect-cost recovery, this is a feature; for taxpayer ROI, it is a gap.
- **"Broker" access to compute (new § 5401(g)(4)(D))** is an interesting hook — small IHEs could use awards to buy time on NAIRR or commercial cloud rather than stand up on-prem clusters, but the section is silent on whether NAIRR credits count.
- **Section-by-section vs. bill body.** The Trahan-office summary describes the target as "institutions not among the top 100 in federal R&D expenditures to broaden participation" — accurate, but elides that MSIs are not statutorily favored. Members should not describe § 221 as an HBCU/MSI program.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / capacity-building:** genuine expansion of AI research capacity beyond the top-100 concentration is a real supply-side lever for the U.S. talent pipeline. The "broker access to compute" pathway is the most operationally useful element — it lets small departments participate without capital expenditure.
- **Equity framing (proceed carefully):** the political packaging leans on HBCUs/HSIs/Tribal Colleges, but the mechanism does not. Advocates who want MSI-targeted funding should push for an MSI set-aside or scoring bonus explicit in statute; without it, awards can flow to non-MSI institutions in the 101–500 R&D range that already have significant infrastructure.
- **Rural / geographic:** "Geographic diversity" (new § 5401(g)(7)(B)) is permissive-only. Rural-district members who want a rural set-aside should note the language is weaker than § 233's Rural and Low-Income Areas AI Collaborative.
- **National security / talent:** consistent with the House AI Task Force's talent-pipeline recommendations. Coalition-friendly with members who worry about PRC AI-talent competition — capacity-building at non-top-100 IHEs directly expands the domestic-trained AI workforce.
