# SEC. 411. INTERNATIONAL COALITIONS ON INNOVATION, DEVELOPMENT, AND ALIGNMENT OF STANDARDS WITH RESPECT TO ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE — summary

**One-line:** Directs DOE and NIST to jointly lead US federal engagement at international AI standards bodies and to form coalitions with like-minded, WTO-TBT-compliant governments to push private-sector-led standards — with the PRC statutorily excluded until USTR certifies WTO compliance in its annual China-compliance report.

## What it does

The Secretary of Energy and the Under Secretary of Commerce for Standards and Technology (NIST) "shall jointly lead information exchange and coordination among agencies and communication from such agencies to private sector entities of the United States and like-minded governments" for the purpose of federal engagement in international AI technical standards (§ 411(a)). They must seek to form alliances or coalitions with foreign governments to pursue seven enumerated activities (§ 411(b)(1)–(7)). Eligibility is gated by jointly-set "technology trust criteria" requiring high scientific/technological advancement and adherence to WTO TBT Annex 2 principles (§ 411(c)), plus a partner-country IP/export-control floor (§ 411(e)). Section 411(f) then hard-codes PRC ineligibility subject to a USTR-plus-interagency two-lock override.

## Key provisions

- **Joint lead:** Sec. of Energy + Under Sec. of Commerce for Standards and Technology (NIST) (§ 411(a)).
- **Seven coalition activities** (§ 411(b)):
  - private-sector-led standards development/adoption (§ 411(b)(1));
  - push adoption of US-developed AI standards by international SDOs (§ 411(b)(2));
  - advocate for governance approaches that "promote innovation and counter influence from foreign adversaries" (§ 411(b)(3));
  - facilitate R&D collaboration including data, expertise, resources (§ 411(b)(4));
  - build government-to-government infrastructure using existing bilateral/multilateral agreements "to the extent practicable" (§ 411(b)(5));
  - identify best practices for cybersecurity of AI models (§ 411(b)(6));
  - coordinate with State, NSC, and NSF on complementary tech-protection measures in research and higher ed (§ 411(b)(7)).
- **Technology trust criteria** jointly set by Sec Commerce + Sec Energy + Sec State + NSF Director (§ 411(c)): high sci/tech advancement (§ 411(c)(1)) and WTO TBT Annex 2 principles — "transparency, openness, and consensus-based decision-making" (§ 411(c)(2)).
- **Partner IP/export-control floor:** partners must have "sufficient intellectual property protections, safety standards, and risk management approaches" and coordinated research-security/export-control/IP measures (§ 411(e)(1)–(2)).
- **PRC exclusion + two-lock override** (§ 411(f)): ineligible *unless* USTR determines PRC WTO-compliance in the annual 22 U.S.C. 6951 report; *and* the NSF Director + Sec Commerce + Sec Energy + Sec State then jointly report to Congress on national security interest, tech-transfer risk, human rights, and monitoring capacity (§ 411(f)(2)(A)(i)–(iv)).
- **Rule of construction:** does not bar private participation in SDOs and does not constrain "separate engagement with emerging economies" (§ 411(g)).

## Who it affects

- **Regulated parties:** None directly. This is diplomatic-coordination authority; no compliance obligations attach to firms.
- **Empowered actors:** DOE Secretary, NIST Under Secretary, Sec Commerce, Sec State, NSF Director, USTR (as gatekeeper on the PRC carve-out), NSC (via § 411(b)(7)). CAISI (§ 102) is the natural NIST-side venue but is not named here.
- **Beneficiaries:** US industry participating at ISO/IEC JTC 1 SC 42, IEEE, ITU, OECD, and Council of Europe FCAI; allied SDO delegations (UK, EU MS, Japan, Korea, Australia, Canada, Israel, India, likely Singapore) that meet § 411(c)/(e) criteria.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms used:** "Artificial intelligence" and "foreign adversary" per § 101; "critical safety incident" indirectly via the § 411(b)(6) cybersecurity language.
- **Depends on / paired with:**
  - § 102 (CAISI) — the domestic NIST-side entity that would deliver technical content to coalition partners.
  - § 248 (voluntary consensus effort for federal workforce-data standards) — same private-sector-led / consensus-driven philosophy, applied domestically rather than at international SDOs.
  - § 301 (CISA 2015 reauth through 2035) — domestic backbone for cyber-threat-indicator sharing; § 411(b)(6) is the international-cooperation arm on cybersecurity of AI models.
  - § 321 (GAO report on model weight security) — the domestic empirical baseline that § 411(b)(6) coalition best-practice work would ideally feed off of.
  - § 431 (research security) — the general research-security regime that § 411(b)(7) plugs into internationally.

## Notable statutory language

> "The People's Republic of China is not eligible to participate in an alliance or coalition under subsection (b) unless the United States Trade Representative determines in a report to Congress required by section 421 of the U.S.-China Relations Act of 2000 (22 U.S.C. 6951) that the People's Republic of China has come into compliance with the commitments made in connection with accession to the World Trade Organization." (§ 411(f)(1))

The verb — "is not eligible … unless" — is a hard exclusion tied to an annual USTR determination that has never issued in favor of compliance since the report's creation in 2001. The "unless" clause is a two-lock structure: even if USTR flipped, the NSF Director + Sec Commerce + Sec Energy + Sec State would still have to jointly clear a national-security/human-rights/monitoring assessment (§ 411(f)(2)) before a PRC-inclusive coalition could form.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **Statutory codification adds a floor, not a new exclusion.** State + Commerce + NIST already coordinate informally to counter PRC influence at ISO/IEC JTC 1 SC 42 (PRC held the SC 42 chair 2018–2024), ITU, and IEEE. § 411(f) does not create the exclusion — it hardens it so a future administration can't quietly re-engage without a USTR reversal + a public NatSec assessment.
- **Downside: brittleness.** Genuine cross-cutting standards work (safety-incident taxonomies, model-evaluation methodology) is often more useful with PRC in the room than out of it — and PRC will remain in ISO/IEC regardless of this coalition mechanism (both are WTO/ISO members). A US-led coalition that excludes PRC risks becoming an ally-side channel that runs *parallel* to ISO rather than shaping it. Whether that is a feature or a bug depends on strategic posture.
- **Multilateral bodies not named.** § 411 speaks of "international standards organizations" generically. ISO/IEC JTC 1 SC 42, IEEE, ITU-T SG 13, OECD AIGO/AI expert group, and Council of Europe FCAI are all plausibly in scope. That flexibility is normal but leaves the coalition's operational shape to executive-branch discretion.
- **"Federal engagement" is coordination, not delegation.** § 411(a) grants information-exchange and communication authority; the actual US delegation to ISO still runs through ANSI, and IEEE participation is by individuals. NIST does not gain speaking-for-the-US authority under this section.
- **"Private sector-led development and adoption" (§ 411(b)(1)) locks in the US model.** This is the industry-led-SDO philosophy — same logic as § 248's "voluntary and, when feasible, consensus-driven effort" — and it is a direct contrast to the EU AI Act's harmonized-standards model in which the Commission mandates deliverables from CEN-CENELEC. § 411 is quietly a soft-power push to keep the US SDO model dominant.
- **Cybersecurity item is one verb-light bullet.** § 411(b)(6) — "identify best practices to maintain the cybersecurity of artificial intelligence models" — is the lightest possible verb. No sharing infrastructure, no mandatory adoption, no coupling to CISA 2015 authority. Compare § 301's operational threat-indicator sharing regime.
- **Human-rights bullet in § 411(f)(2)(A)(iii) is present but non-operative.** It only fires *if* USTR certifies PRC compliance, which is not going to happen; and even then it's a "how officials would intend to address" clause, not a bar.
- **USTR review standard is not specified.** § 411(f)(1) simply defers to the existing 22 U.S.C. 6951 report; there is no interagency consultation requirement on the USTR side, and no standard-of-review for Congress. The § 411(f)(2) joint report is separate and post-hoc.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** This section is a soft-power arm of the "keep the US industry-led SDO model dominant" agenda. If EU-style mandated standards win the international competition, US regulatory frameworks (including much of the rest of GAAIA) become second-mover; § 411 is meant to prevent that. Congressional supporters of federal preemption will read § 411 as the outward-facing counterpart to their domestic no-patchwork argument.
- **National security:** The China exclusion is the headline. Genuine value-add is the statutory hard-floor against future re-engagement + the signaling to allies. Cost is brittleness on cross-cutting technical work where PRC exclusion is counterproductive.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Light load. Coalition mechanisms shape governance-of-standards, not safety-of-models. § 411(b)(6) is the closest touch and it is not operational.
- **Free speech / civil liberties:** § 411(f)(2)(A)(iii)'s human-rights nod is real but non-operative — worth mentioning to civil-society coalitions without overselling.
