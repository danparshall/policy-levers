# SEC. 422. FEDERAL GRAND CHALLENGES IN ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE — summary

**One-line:** Adds a new § 5107 to the National AI Initiative Act directing OSTP to stand up a prize-competition program across 14 named AI R&D priorities (chip design, interpretability, safety, manufacturing, border security, science, cybersecurity, energy, legacy-code modernization, and more), with no dollar authorization and a 5-year sunset.

## What it does

New NAII Act § 5107(a) directs the OSTP Director, acting through the National Science and Technology Council and the Interagency Committee, to establish — within one year of enactment — a prize competition program using the general prize authority at **§ 24 of the Stevenson-Wydler Technology Innovation Act (15 U.S.C. 3719)** (not America COMPETES; worth noting since Stevenson-Wydler § 24 caps individual prize purses at $50M without Congressional notification). OSTP, in consultation with industry, civil society, and academia, sets and annually updates the priority list; per-competition problem statements, success metrics, and awards must be posted on Challenge.gov (§ 5107(b)(3)). Grand-challenge selection is coordinated with NIST and DARPA (§ 5107(b)(4)). § 5107(c) additionally authorizes Commerce, DOT, and NSF to run their own challenge-based acquisitions on the same priority list.

## Key provisions

- **14 permitted priority areas (§ 5107(b)(2)(A)–(N)):** (A) microelectronics / chip engineering incl. neuromorphic and quantum; (B) transformational computing/AI advances — algorithm design, next-gen compute, generative AI for design, photonics/electrophotonics, new materials chemistry, biotech (single-cell modeling), energy efficiency, cryptographic content provenance, **AI safety and controls**; (C) **explainability and mechanistic interpretability**; (D) fundamental AI understanding — interpretability, control, steerability, robustness against foreign adversaries; (E) advanced manufacturing; (F) maritime AI (navigation, propulsion, fuels); (G) **border security (fentanyl, contraband, illegal activities)**; (H) AI for science; (I) cybersecurity for AI IP; (J) modernizing federal / critical-infrastructure legacy code; (K) reducing AI energy consumption; (L) preventing malicious misuse of AI; (M) wireless / cellular applications; (N) AI for physical and cloud laboratories.
- **No authorization of appropriations, no minimum prize purse specified.** The section creates authority to spend, not a budget.
- **GAO oversight (§ 422(b)):** initial study on prize competitions generally, reported at 18 months; interim study on this program at 42 months, both with cost-effectiveness and counterfactual (would grants/contracts have produced the same result) elements.
- **Notification (§ 5107(d)(1))** within 60 days of any award to House SST and Senate Commerce; biennial public program report (§ 5107(d)(2)).
- **5-year sunset (§ 5107(e)).**

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** OSTP Director (lead); NIST, DARPA, "other relevant" agency heads (grand-challenge selection); Secretary of Commerce, Secretary of Transportation, NSF Director (companion challenge-based acquisitions).
- **Beneficiaries:** U.S. AI R&D community — the "eligible participants" clause defers to Stevenson-Wydler § 24 eligibility; § 5107(c)(2)(B) requires all funded research to be conducted in the United States.

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms:** "agency" = 5 U.S.C. § 403 Executive agency; "critical infrastructure" = USA PATRIOT ACT § 1016 (42 U.S.C. 5195c) (§ 5107(f)).
- **Paired with:** § 421 (public federal datasets for AI), § 423 (NAIRR compute/data resource — the substrate a serious grand-challenge team would need), § 424 (liquid-cooling GAO review). Prize-competition machinery also appears in § 245 (NIST benchmark prize) and § 253 (NSF forecasting prize) — those carry actual dollar authorizations; § 422 does not.

## Notable statutory language

> "To promote explainability and mechanistic interpretability of artificial intelligence systems." (§ 5107(b)(2)(C))

> "To develop artificial intelligence solutions to improve border security, including solutions relevant to the detection of fentanyl, illicit contraband, and illegal activities." (§ 5107(b)(2)(G))

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **No dollar figure = weak lever.** Prize competitions' track record is bimodal: Ansari X ($10M), DARPA Grand Challenge ($2M), and ARC Prize ($1M+) moved fields; sub-$500k Challenge.gov prizes routinely produce demos rather than technology. Without an authorization or a floor, § 422 could be run as a $50k/year ornament.
- **"Interpretability" appears three times** (§ 5107(b)(2)(B)(ix) safety/controls, § 5107(b)(2)(C) explainability + mechanistic interpretability, § 5107(b)(2)(D)(i) as a fundamental-understanding priority). Substantive alignment/safety framing landing in a bipartisan discussion draft is worth surfacing — it survived Republican drafting.
- **Border security (G) is politically load-bearing.** It reads as a hand to CBP/DHS constituencies for cross-aisle support. Substantively narrow (fentanyl + contraband detection), but note that placement on the priority list is discretionary — OSTP is not required to include (G) in any given year.
- **"May include" not "shall include" (§ 5107(b)(2))** — OSTP has full discretion to drop any priority in any annual update. The 14-priority list is a menu, not a mandate.
- **Definitions are thin.** No definition of "grand challenge" itself; no minimum prize purse; "eligible participants" undefined here and inherited from Stevenson-Wydler.
- **Sunset at 5 years (§ 5107(e))** without a reauthorization pathway; GAO interim report lands at 42 months, cutting reauthorization runway to ~18 months.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Genuine win. Mechanistic interpretability, control, steerability, and misuse-prevention (L) are named in statute — the first federal grand-challenge program to do so. Framing point: this is bipartisan legislative endorsement that interpretability is a national R&D priority. The soft spot is execution — no dollar authorization means the interpretability priority competes annually with 13 others for a bucket of money that Congress has not sized.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Prize competitions are a low-regulatory-burden R&D lever and fit the Obernolte-side "compete, don't regulate" framing. Companion challenge-based acquisitions at Commerce/DOT/NSF (§ 5107(c)) broaden the surface area beyond OSTP.
- **National security:** (D)(iv) "robustness against foreign adversaries" and (I) cybersecurity for AI IP are the explicit national-security hooks. (G) border security lands adjacent. China not named but implied.
- **Worker / labor:** (E) advanced manufacturing worker-safety subpriority and (K) energy efficiency touch labor-adjacent concerns; the section is not a workforce vehicle.
