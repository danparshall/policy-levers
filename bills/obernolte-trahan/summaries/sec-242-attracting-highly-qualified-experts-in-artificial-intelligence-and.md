# SEC. 242. ATTRACTING HIGHLY QUALIFIED EXPERTS IN AI AND OTHER FIELDS — summary

**One-line:** Gives the Secretary of Labor a narrow, capped, and sunsetting excepted-service hiring authority — up to 20 AI/ML/data-science experts on 24-month appointments, base pay at GS-15 step 10 but aggregate comp allowed up to VP-salary — restricted to labor-market forecasting duties and funded at only $6M over five years.

## What it does

Creates a bespoke Title 5 workaround so DOL can bring in AI expertise without going through the competitive-service hiring pipeline. The Secretary may appoint "covered individuals" — persons with expertise in AI, ML, advanced data science, or a related field (§ 242(b)(1)(B)) — directly into excepted-service positions at DOL (§ 242(b)(2)), waiving Chapter 33 examination/selection, Chapter 51 classification, and Subchapter III of Chapter 53 pay rules (§ 242(e)). Appointments run up to 24 months, extendable once for up to 24 additional months on a Secretary certification to Congress and the President that the individual "continues to be highly valuable" (§ 242(f)).

## Key provisions

- **Headcount cap:** No more than 20 employees simultaneously serving under this section (§ 242(h)).
- **Basic pay:** Up to GS-15 step 10 (§ 242(c)(1)) — ~$195K in DC locality.
- **Aggregate comp ceiling:** Notwithstanding 5 U.S.C. § 5307, basic pay + locality + recruitment/relocation/retention incentives + performance awards may not exceed VP salary per 3 U.S.C. § 104 (§ 242(c)(3)) — ~$285K. Section-by-section text ("GS-15 pay") elides this ceiling.
- **Narrow duties:** Primarily (1) measurement/forecasting of AI's impact on labor markets and workforce training needs, and (2) policymaking for labor-market adjustment (§ 242(g)). Not general-purpose DOL AI capacity.
- **4-year sunset:** Appointment authority terminates 4 years after enactment unless reauthorized; incumbents already appointed keep serving (§ 242(k)).
- **Appropriations:** $6M total for FY2026–2030 (§ 242(l)) — ~$1.2M/yr.
- **Preserves suitability + security clearance requirements** (§ 242(j)); no Senate confirmation (these are excepted-service, not political, appointments).
- **Annual report** to Senate HELP and House Ed & Workforce on headcount, qualifications, and impact (§ 242(i)).

## Who it affects

- **Empowered actors:** Secretary of Labor (all appointment discretion vests here).
- **Beneficiaries:** DOL's Employment and Training Administration and BLS-adjacent workforce policy shops — the natural home for the § 242(g) forecasting duties. Overlaps with the AI Workforce Research Hub (§ 243) and BLS voluntary-reporting program (§ 246).

## Cross-references

- **Defined terms:** "competitive service" and "excepted service" are pulled directly from 5 U.S.C. §§ 2102–2103 (§ 242(b)(1)(A), (C)); "covered individual" defined in-line at § 242(b)(1)(B). This section does not use the § 101 bill-wide definitions.
- **Depends on / paired with:** § 243 (AI Workforce Research Hub) and §§ 244, 246, 247 (labor-market data infrastructure) — the § 242 hires are the technical staffing this Subtitle assumes.

## Drafting notes & open questions

- **The appropriation cannot fund the cap.** $6M / 5 years ≈ $1.2M/yr. 20 FTEs at $195K base = $3.9M/yr salary alone before incentives, benefits, or overhead. The authorization funds roughly 5–6 FTEs at cap comp, not 20. Either the headcount cap is aspirational or DOL will absorb the balance from base appropriations.
- **GS-15 base is far below market for senior AI talent.** Frontier-lab senior researchers clear $500K–$1M+ total comp. The (c)(3) aggregate ceiling at VP salary (~$285K) helps at the margin — retention incentives can roughly double base — but this authority will not, standing alone, staff DOL with the caliber the "highly qualified" language implies. Realistic recruits are mid-career experts on public-service sabbaticals or academics.
- **Schedule ambiguity.** The section creates excepted-service positions without specifying Schedule A/B/C/D/E. Given the recent Schedule F / "Schedule Policy/Career" instability (revived by EO in 2025), the drafters' silence leaves it to OPM to slot these appointments. Worth watching whether they land somewhere insulated from at-will removal.
- **Structural asymmetry with CAISI (§ 102).** CAISI hiring authority is *uncapped in headcount* and reaches VP salary via 3 U.S.C. § 104; § 102(b) also repeals the 15-person cap on NIST critical-technical hires. DOL under § 242 is capped at 20, appropriated at $6M/5yr, and sunsets at 4 years. Commerce's AI workforce is being sized as a standing institution; Labor's is being sized as a small pilot. Given that AI labor-market impact is the largest single vector the bill's Title II purports to address, the resourcing asymmetry is load-bearing.

## Policy conversation angles

- **Worker / labor:** The mismatch between the scale of the AI-labor-impact problem and the size of this hiring authority (20 people, capped, sunsetting, underfunded) is the substantive story. This is the only Title II hiring provision for DOL AI expertise; if it doesn't fund out, the workforce-impact analytical capacity in §§ 243–248 has no owner.
- **Innovation / anti-patchwork:** Not loaded — § 242 is internal federal hiring, not regulatory.
- **Safety / catastrophic-risk:** Not loaded — duties are labor-market analytical, not safety.
