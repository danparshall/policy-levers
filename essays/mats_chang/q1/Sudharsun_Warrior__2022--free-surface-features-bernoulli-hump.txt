ABSTRACT- Submarines are known to produce certain surface manifestations, primary among 
them being a feature known as Berno ulli Hump (BH). BH is a rise and fall of sea surface height 
above the submarine, when the vessel cruises close to surface. This feature has been noted by 
many researchers but scant information is publically available on the same due to the classified 
nature of the data.  A major part of the Wide Area Surveillance program for submarines of the 
world navies uses the detection of this BH . In this work, our objecti ve is to detect and establis h 
the presence of BH characteristics. After calibrating a known CFD code using experimental data, 
we make model simulations which clearly depict the BH and its features. We then show the path 
ahead for submarine detection using BH  from observations of sea surface heights. Value  of BH 
height for a submarine at 10m depth moving at 5 knots is about 20cm which falls within the 2 cm 
vertical resolution of altimeters. Though most of the  studies were for calm waters, an 
introduction to the presence of regular wind waves is given at the end.   
 
KEYWORDS: WAS, SAR, CFD, submarines and wake signature.

1. INTRODUCTION   
WAS is now adays a term inology rotuninely used for national security. WAS indicates the 
surveillance of l arge swaths of area using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)  images from air -
borne/space borne  satellites ( Kanjir et al., 2018) . Western nations have been using this 
technology to detect unwarranted moving of targets in land and water. Most of this air -
surveillance was done by Army to keep up with the transport and movement of trucks and other 
heavy enemy vehicles on land. Then later, this technolo gy made its foray into the marine 
surveillance whereby SAR images  are used to track ships/unauthorized vessels at sea (Deng et 
al., 2018).  
Similar technique can also be used to track submarines. When a submarine moves at 40 m 
depth or less, it leaves  a trail in the surface above . Very little work is done on this s urface 
manifestation, the biggest contribution came from  the work of Tunaley, 20 15. He has used a 
Rankine ovoid method and by describing the submarine as a collection of sources and sinks, 
predicted the presence of Bernoulli Hump. But after that, literature is scant on this area, probably 
due to the sensitive nature of the data. Inspite of the fact that the submarine itself can be detected 
directly, there are two main reasons why it is a better idea to detect the vessel’s wake and not the 
vessel itself: the wake pattern is longer and clearer than the submarine itself in the image, and it 
can yield a better estimate of the submarine’s actual position and velocity(Chen et al., 2008). 
From the historical perspective, the trail left by ducks/boats when they sail through water has 
fascinated man because of the beau tiful wake signature behind it (Thomson W, 1887) . It was 
Lord Kelvin, who put scientific rigor  to the phenomenon by demonstrating  that the angle made 
by the wake of any object moving in water is the same, irrespective of the object itself.   This 
wake now called as Kelvin wake is a combination of two wave fields - transverse waves and 
divergent waves. This wave  system is found to converge at an angle of 39o (Raboud et al., 2013) 
at the position of ship.    
Synthetic Aperture Radar ( SAR) images of ship wakes  show a wake in the form of dark and 
bright streaks (Tunaley et al., 1991) . Dark streaks represent the Kelvin wake and bright streaks 
represent turbulent wake (Fig 1).

Initially considered as a feat ure of beauty, it was later recognised  that a wake signature is a 
source of hidden inform ation, helping detection of ship ’s particulars  (Peregrine et al 1971) . 
Detection by extracting the information from Kelvin wake first was investigated by Newman, 
1991. This was followed up by Griffins (Griffins et al., 1996) . While Newman deals with 
estimates of the ship hull cross-section parameters (not the length),  in Griffins only the estimates 
of the ship length are examined. An algebraic expansion for the width of the turbulent wake aft a 
cargo ship with zero axial net momentum was predicted in ( Birkhoff et al, 1957), but only after 
quite some time it has been verified for other vessels (Peltzer etal.,1991 ). Unfortunately, the 
simple formula give n in (Birkhoff et al, 1957)  for the width of the turbulent wake aft a ship is 
purely asymptotic, i.e., it does not have a length-scale and, therefore, cannot be used for 
estimating the ship particulars . By  invoking (Birkhoff et al, 1957) hypothesis, (Zilman et al., 
1999, Zilman et al., 2004) derived a functional relation between the ship breadth and the width of 
its turbulent wake.  
On the other hand, a  lot of study on underwater vessels center around AUVs.  Numerous 
authors have studied underwater bodies for understanding the hydrodynamic forces and moments 
on it  (Jagadeesh et al., 2009;  Saeidinezhad et al., 2015). Jagadeesh et al., 2009  has made 
experimental studies of hydrodynamic derivatives and other coefficients of an AUV hull 
form. Saeidinezhad et al. (2015)  has studied the pitching of AUVs and drawn out the 
hydrodynamic coefficients associated with it . A lot of recent studies have been using CFD high 
fidelity simulations to capture the fl ow around the hull.  The accuracy of turbulence model, the 
discretization of the equations and meshing  thus play a vital role here (Sakthivel et al. 2011).

Most of the studies on submarines have been done for maneuvering and measurements of 
hydrodynamic derivatives. This is mostly because of the importance of proper maneuvering 
characteristics for submarines ( Feldman, 1987, Quick et al., 2014, Dubbioso et al 2016). People 
have done captive model tests, PMM tests and rotating  arm tests to measure these coefficients. 
Recently, there has been a surge in the application of CFD simulations in these tests.  Tyagi and 
Sen, 2006  employed a RANS solver to study the horizontal hydrodynamic coefficients of an 
AUV.  Wu et al.,2005  used a SUBOFF model to simulate a steady line motion with and without 
an angle of attack . The near -bottom effects on the hydrodynamic characteristics  were 
investigated. Hu et al. 2007 used the CFX software to simulate the maneuvering tests  by using 
the k-ε model to simulate the hydrodynamic derivatives. 
Broglia et al.2007,  used CFD simulations to study the sway characteristics of a KVLCC 
tanker with free surface effects.  The motion of the vessel was simulated using an overlapping 
mesh method with 8 blocks  for a fixed background and 20 fitted blocks moving with the hull. 
The computed lateral force and the yaw moment agreed well with the experimental data with a 
relative error less than 5.5% and 20%, respectively. Yang et al. , 2011 investigated a similar 
phenomenon without considering free surface effects. 
This paper aims to study a possible detection  technique using CFD software. It may be 
possible to detect a submarine using accurate measurements of the ocean surface height or by 
imaging the water surface. The major physical feature will be the wake developed by the 
underwater body in motion. Three different phenomena are caused either directly or indirectly by 
surface wakes: internal waves and turbulence, Kelvin wake and Bernoulli’s Hump. In thi s study 
Bernoulli’s hump is the main area of focus. Some of the original works made by (Hershey, 1966) 
is based on Rankine ovoid model, which is also called “Doublet” based on single source and 
sink. The same meth odology is applied by (Stefanick, 1987) in which he pre pared a table for 
various size of Bernoulli’s hump created by various submarine type s. Later (Daly, 1994) 
produced this table in his study of submarine detectability.  
Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) is a vast area of research that was traditionally in the 
Mechanical Engineering discipline. But in the last few decades, there has been a massive 
breakthrough in practical applications of CFD towards ship hydrodynamics (Z hang et al., 2010; 
Kundu et al., 2019) . CFD can play a valuable role in the investigation and prediction of ship 
performance. Model testing, which has always been the traditional technique for accurate

prediction of any ship flow, is more costly and time -consuming as compared to computational

Barman et al. 2018, a RANS code  was used to make predictions of ship wakes and a large

version 2019.3) . Then numerical analysis was performed using that calibrated model to simulate 
various test cases of depth of operation and velocity of submarine models.

2. MATERIALS AND METHODS 
2.1 Background 
The prescribed method of detecting underwater bodies is by two techniques: direct and 
indirect techniques. In direct technique, the vessel itself is detected; whereas in indirect 
method, any physical or environmental disturbance caused by the vessel is det ected as a 
proxy for the vessel itself. 
Direct Detection: In this case, the light reflected from the submerged vessel is used to detect 
the vessel. Usually the blue -green (450- 550nm) band of the spectrum is used, as it is less 
absorbed by ocean itself. 
Indirect Detection : All other methods of detection are by indirect means. These are 
classified into: physical surface effects, optical effects and thermal effects.  
Physical Surface Effects: The major physical surface effect is to detect the wake due to the 
vessel. The wake can be classified into Kelvin wake, Bernoulli Hump and internal waves. 
The Bernoulli Hump:  When the submarine travels underwater, it produces a vivid rise and 
fall of sea surface height above it. This feature is known as Bernoulli Hump and  it is a 
function of velocity, depth of operation and dimensions of the vessel. The height of the hump 
can vary from tens of centimeters near the surface to a few mm at a depth of about 50 m  
(Stephanick et al. 1988). 
 Kelvin Waves :  These  waves were discu ssed in detail in the introduction section and we 
donot elaborate it here again.

Numerical simulations were performed for these equations using STARCCM+ to study the 
wake formation on the surface of the water when submarine moves underneath . These RANS 
simulations employ Volume of Fluid method for multiphase flows, to capture the distur bance at 
interface of air and water, accurately. The STARCCM+ software is used with the k- ε turbulence 
model to solve the transport equations for turbulent  kinetic energy and the rate of dissipation of 
turbulent kinetic energy. Semi-implicit method for pressure linked equation scheme(SIMPLE) is 
used to solve the discretized equations.  
Numerical simulations were performed on bare hull, for investigation of submarine signatures 
at a velocity of 5 and 10 knots (2.5 and 5 m/s). The time step size for convergence of the solution 
has been taken 0.01 sec. Once numerical solution s converged, disturbance caused by moving 
vessel was captured in the imagery . These images helped in developing the complete picture of 
the Bernoulli Hump.

Meshing is done in such a way that cells are carefully distributed in the region surrounding the 
submarine. The complete analysis is done in full -scale. The base size of the grid is set at L/50, 
where L is the length of the submarine. The mesh size has been fixed after a series of mesh 
convergence iterations performed. This mesh size is as recommended by the ITTC standards (see 
Fig 2b). Trimmed cell mesher is a volume mesher and is primarily used to discretize the domain. 
Prism Layers are used around the submarine body in order to capture the boundary layer that is 
formed submarine, by the virtue o f viscosity. The free surface is also discretized into smaller 
cells to capture the free surface elevation distinctly. For different depths of operations, the 
refinement regions and submarine model are translated downwards. Refined meshes are provided 
along the pressure points of the submarine to capture the flow variations induced by the 
submarine. Sufficient damping factors are provided from the boundaries until the refined mesh 
spaces so as to dampen any unnecessary wave reflections. Thus, we have a total of about 2 to 2.5 
million cells, varying based on the different models employed and different operating conditions.

3. RESULTS 
3.1 STARCCM+ Calibration 
The primary prob lem of detecting  the very faint Bernoulli Hump in the laboratory forced us  to 
adopt the following method. We prepared the model of an underwater vessel as described in last 
section and tested it. We measured the variation of turbulent kinetic energy and normal and 
tangential Reynolds stresses. Then a STARCCM+ model was calibrated to those results (shown 
below).  After this, we scaled up the numerical model to f ull-scale submarine dime nsions and 
test-simulated it for various conditions. The Bernoulli Hump was noticed in the simulations 
above the position of the submarine. The model was tested for various depths of operation and 
velocities of operation. We present these results.  Fig 6a -d shows the comparison of turbulent 
kinetic energy between model results and experimental values. The circles represent the 
experiment values whereas the curves represent STARCCM+ results (with and without wal l 
reflection terms). As can be seen, the values are matching to high degee of accuracy.  The x axis 
represents the non-dimensionalized (with radius of vessel) distance along the hull (readings just 
near the hull).

===== page 20 =====

20 
 
Model was calibrated for this experiment b y using the STARCCM+ software . The numerical 
model was calibrated with a linear pressure strain model, for slow pressure strain correlations. 
Two cases were investigated, the presence of slow and rapid pressure strain and one more with 
the presence of wall  reflection term. Fig 6a- d show the values of non- dimensionalized TKE and 
normal and tangential Reynolds stresses  from both numerical and experimental methods . It was 
clear that including the wall reflection term improved the results  (Gibson and Launder, 1978). 
Fig 6b shows how the energy transfer occurs between the stream -wise and transverse 
components of flow.  So for all our submarine simulations also, we considered the wall reflection 
term near the surface. 
The model was thus calibrated for domain of simulation, grids (meshes), turbulence  
model and boundary conditions, and then we chose to run for the two full-scale submarines. 
 
3.1.1 Boundary conditions for CFD 
 
While simulating in full scale, more number of cells are required to capture the Bernoulli 
hump. Since the submarine is symmetric about centreline, it is reasonable to assume that flow is 
symmetric. Hence only one half of the fluid domain is modeled. This reduces the number of cells 
resulting in reduced computational time. The domain and boundary conditions of full -scale 
simulation are shown in figure5.1.  
The volume mesh consists of hexahedrons in an unstructured grid. The mesh is generated 
using the trimmer-mesh function in STAR -CCM+. Prism layer mesh is used on the hull surface 
in order to make a reasonable estimation of the shear stresses on the hull. Free- Surface is also 
refined to capture the Bernoulli hump.

4.1  Way ahead for detection of submarines: 
As the previous sections show, submarine leave s barely perceptible signatures on the ocean 
surface. When the vessel is travelling at 10 knots speed and about 20 m below water, it produces 
a small (about 15 cm) but measurable Bernoulli Hump. This is measurable by the current 
altimeter resolutions. This rise and fallof sea surface height is shown in Fig 10  below. The ocean 
surface as viewed from above (like from a SAR on an airplane) has been recreated in that figure. 
We can see a perceptible feature at the surface for 20m depth of operation with a BH of 10- 15 
cm. Beyond that, the chances of detection are low. Over and above this, i f sea is rough, these 
features wouldn’t be vis ible at all. So our work is for calm waters only. For rougher seas, the 
SAR image wil have to be filtered out to remove the wind waves for the BH to be visible. This is

4.2 BH in presence of regular waves: 
It is a known fact that ocean can never be in a totally calm state without environmental waves. 
Such waves can be imposed on a submarine wake thus, totally obscuring the BH. To detect and 
bring to use the properties of BH  in such cases, we need to filter out these environmenta l waves. 
Ocean exists as a spectrum of waves (like JONSWAP, Pierson -Moskowich or Bretschneider 
spectrum).  Conventionally, t he significant wave heights and periods are forecasted f rom the 
wind information on the basis of the wind –wave relationship. However, the error may become 
large due to many uncertainties in the wind generation prediction and wind–wave relationship..

===== page 31 =====

31 
 
This paper is mainly focused on calm -wave conditions and we have only made a foray into the 
presence of environmental waves. Quite a bit of work is already completed, but mainly in terms 
of regular waves. Regular waves can easily be filtered out by using a filter on FFT analysis.Thus 
the periodic waves are removed b y FFT filtering. The purpose of doing filtering is to get the BH 
in calm water, which can then be used for reverse calculation of submarine particulars  (work 
under progress). This, we include in the paper.  
Here, the waves and Bernoulli hump exist  merged in the space domain. Wavenumber 
distribution of the space domain is found by Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). By using the Band -
stop filter of appropriate range, filtering is done on the wavenumber domain. Then, Inverse FFT 
is applied to the filtered wavenumber domain, to get the wave subtracted free surface in which 
only BH is present. If there is more than one wave, the same procedure of applying Band -stop 
filters to the wavenumber domain can be followed.  Thus, for these regular waves, FFT is 
employed on  the sea surface elevations. To do FFT, the freesurface should be uniform grid 
spacings, which it is (Fig 2b). Currently we have done it only for regular waves at the surface. 
Figure below shows the wave cut at the centerline of the submarine in the fr ee-surface with a 
simulated Bernoulli H ump of submarine moving at 5.144 m/s at the water depth of 10 m with 
waves of wavelength 80 m and wave amplitude 0.1 m.  Filtering was done using the 2D FFT 
routine of Matlab. 
 The final goal of these studies is to reverse-calculate the details of the submarine from the 
features of Bernoulli Hump (its depth variation and and length). For this, it is enough if we study 
a longitudinal cut along the length of the submarine at the free surface above the centerline. We 
measure the BH parameters along that line which can then be fed into reverse- calculation 
algorithms. So we show the Fig 12 for that wave -cut only. But this can easily be extended to the 
whole surface. 
Procedure 
1. Obtain the free surface elevations of ocean wav es along the wave cut  where the wave 
subtraction has to be done (Fig 12). 
2. Apply Fast Fourier Transforms on the free surface and plot  wave number distribution of 
waves in it.

4. Apply Inverse Fast Fourier Transforms on the filtered wavenumber distribution, to

5. CONCLUSIONS 
In this paper, we use an indirect method of detecting submarines by processing the 
environmental anomalies produced while the vessel is near the surface. Numerical and 
experimental investigations were performed sucessfully and it is concluded that: 
• Both experimental and numerical analysis  converged towards accuracy and shows that 
the detection of submarines using these techniques is robust and stable. 
• In the numercal simulations, two submarines of Indi an Navy (Akula class submarine and 
the Sindhughosh class submarine) have been taken for study. These two submarines have 
been simulated for near-surface motion at 5 knots and 10 knots respectively.  At 10 knots 
speed, the Akula submarine shows a BH of 162 cm which can easily be detected by SAR. 
By the time it goes to 20 m depth, the BH height is only about 15-18 cm.  
• BH can also be seen in the presence of environmental waves. The wind waves need to be 
filtered out by FFT filtering using Matlab toolbox. 
• A false image of sea surface elevations has been created for Akula class submarine for 
various dept hs of oper ation. This is what we would see from SAR images. Image 
processing of the SAR images can therefore be a method of detection of the submarine.  
• Reverse calculation schemes are currently underway to study the submarine particulars 
from the above SAR images.
