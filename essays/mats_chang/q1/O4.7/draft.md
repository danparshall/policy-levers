# AI and nuclear stability: four channels of breakdown

The question asks for a taxonomy of scenarios in which the load-bearing assumptions of nuclear stability might break down under continued AI progress, counterfactually resulting in nuclear war. The operative clause is "assume AI progress continues," and I want to be honest that this is where most of the analytic work happens. A survey calibrated to the current state of AI is a survey of the pre-takeoff regime, and the pre-takeoff regime is the regime the Foreign Affairs essay by Sam Winter-Levy and Nikita Lalwani analyzes carefully and mostly correctly. My argument is that under the question's operative clause the FA analysis is right about the physics-locked capability layer and inverts the weighting between capability and perception on everything above it, and that the four-channel taxonomy below is what falls out when you take Chang's clause seriously.

The four channels are the targeting channel, the decision channel, the NC3-belief channel, and the prize channel. The first three attack information conditions of stability; the fourth changes the payoff matrix and is not present in the FA analysis at all. I will take them in that order, compare against FA, and close on the takeoff-regime hedge where the taxonomy itself becomes brittle.

## Targeting

The classical survivability argument for TELs and SSBNs runs through concealment inside a weapon's time-of-flight. Ignorance is the armor. The relevant physical decomposition of what can and cannot be found has three bins, and it took me some working through to see them cleanly. Kinematic and geographic limits are unmovable: an ICBM's boost time, its ballistic arc, the ocean's depth, the earth's curvature. Processing limits are movable by AI within an adversarial-classifier ceiling, because clutter rejection in a transparent sensor band is not a clean signal-recovery problem, it is a fight against a heavy-tailed and adversarially-injectable clutter distribution at a near-zero acceptable false-alarm rate. Accrual limits are movable only by geometry, i.e., by adding sensor viewpoints, which cheap launch buys. Each bin has real headroom and each has a real floor, and the FA authors' argument mostly lives at the kinematic and processing floors while the erosion mostly lives in the accrual bin.

The clean base rate for mobile-launcher survivability is the 1991 Scud hunt: roughly 1,500 dedicated coalition sorties, zero confirmed mobile-launcher kills, against a mediocre adversary in open desert, with ground truth continuously established by missiles landing in Tel Aviv. That is a clean experiment because it strips out the "no WMD" confound of 2003 and leaves you with the pure kill-chain arithmetic. The lesson was that DSP satellites detected launches in real time but sensor-to-shooter time exceeded the TEL's displacement time, so every prosecution arrived at warm dirt. The gap has since been attacked systematically. NRO deployed thirteen classified missions in roughly 20 months under the proliferated architecture, ~200 satellites in orbit, collection-to-distribution timelines shortened from hours to single-digit minutes. Space Systems Command awarded SpaceX $4.16 billion in May 2026 for the Space-Based Airborne Moving Target Indicator program. NRO's director framed the mission out loud: "we're making it harder for our adversaries to hide, while reducing time to insights for our customers from minutes to seconds." That framing is itself a strategic input regardless of whether the sensing layer closes the wartime kill chain, because adversary planners must hedge against your claimed capability.

FA's response to this line is to enumerate countermeasures: netting, decoys, tunnels, jammers, injected false information. All real, all deployed at some level, and none of them change the accrual arithmetic. A TEL that shoot-and-scoots buys survival on the gap between satellite passes; as inter-satellite gap time falls with constellation density, the survivable displacement window falls with the square of transit time. That is a scaling result, not an assertion, and the direction of travel is what matters for a stability analysis. FA does not run the arithmetic and does not concede the direction.

The submarine leg is the other half of the story, and here I want to push against my own instinct rather than FA's. Seawater is EM-opaque at any band a satellite can carry, so no orbital sensor sees a hull at patrol depth. The searcher's only handles are surface expressions: internal-wave wakes, the Bernoulli hump, thermal scarring, mast exposures, and above all the cueing of acoustic assets by tracking everything around a submarine (port egress, chokepoint transits). Bernoulli-hump scaling for an Ohio-class hull runs roughly as η ∼ V²D³/(g·d³), where d is depth: at 30 m an Ohio produces a ~0.4 m hump, at 100 m ~1 cm, at 200 m ~1.5 mm, at 300 m sub-millimeter under meters of ambient chop. The searcher who buys a super-resolution factor of ten via processing gets a linear gain; the evader who doubles depth costs the searcher a factor of eight. That is the physics-lock FA is right about, and it holds against every processing gain I can imagine.

But the physics-lock is asymmetric, and the asymmetry is what drives the destabilizing direction. Trident D5's range makes US patrol boxes tens of millions of km² of deep, stratification-messy open ocean: unlayerable, unlockable, safe. China's 094s operate from a Hainan bastion with constrained approaches, chokepoints, and a US sensor architecture already concentrated there: layerable, lockable, exposed. The ocean does not go transparent uniformly. It goes transparent in the direction that threatens the weaker deterrent first, which reads from Beijing's chair as the US approaching damage-limitation capability, which feeds the buildup. The 300-plus new silo fields are, among other things, a bet that the shell game works better in hardened holes than on roads once the roads are watched. FA reports the constraints correctly and does not report the asymmetry, which is the stability-relevant fact.

One more move on the targeting channel, because I think FA underestimates one specific mechanism: decoy discrimination in transparent bands. FA's counter is that ML classifiers can be spoofed and adversarially fooled, which is true and known. The move I want to add is that fusion over cheap ubiquitous incidental data can extract signals that look physics-forbidden at any single measurement. Uber produced a road-quality map covering every 50 m of American roads using only accelerometer traces from driver phones. No single measurement carries any signal; the fused product carries a signal that would have been an intelligence coup fifteen years ago. Whether that class of trick opens on decoy discrimination depends on what fusion inputs the adversary cannot control, which is a real question. It is not settled, and FA settles it prematurely.

## Decision

Schelling's *Strategy of Conflict* observes that in an imperfect decision system, a threat that leaves something to chance is a stability resource. That result gets rediscovered every decade and stated newly by whichever generation is negotiating whichever technology; the mechanism is that if a leader's operational commitment is manifestly beyond his own reliable control, then his threat can be genuinely credible without requiring that he prefer to execute it. Military operational preferences (pre-delegation of launch authority, pre-scripted response, launch-on-warning, dead-hand systems) are commitment devices in the Schelling sense. Civilian ratification lag is a safety measure that partially reverses the commitment. This tension is structural, not a bug, and Schelling is explicit that a government cannot even advertise its own fallibility without dissolving the credibility that the fallibility generates.

AI accelerates every military-preferred move. Faster evaluation shrinks the ratification window. Pre-scripted response becomes cheaper to author, rehearse, and audit. Dead-hand becomes technically tractable in a way it was not when Perimetr was designed. Human-in-the-loop empirically collapses to human-as-ratifier, the automation-bias literature converging on this from labs and cockpits over three decades. The Biden-Xi November 2024 joint statement affirming human control of launch decisions was worth extracting from Beijing precisely because it was slipping under precisely this pressure.

The critical shift is that LOW becomes ADOPTABLE by states that could not previously execute it. Pakistan and DPRK are the load-bearing cases. Neither has the C3 infrastructure to run a genuine LOW posture with acceptable false-alarm discipline; both have every incentive to adopt it as capability erodes. AI-assisted early warning plus AI-drafted response templates lower the technical threshold below their reach. The stability implication is that the second-tier nuclear states move first because they have the fewest resources to adapt any other way, and the second-tier states are also the ones for which the assessment pipeline is thinnest.

Fable pointed out to me a corollary I would not have caught otherwise. The accrual bin from the targeting section says the discriminating information does not exist in the photons yet during early flight: ICBM vs. space launch, attack azimuth vs. benign, cannot be classified until the trajectory has unfolded. A faster evaluator does not get the right answer sooner. It gets a confident answer sooner. AI decision support in an LOW posture is a machine for producing confident wrong classifications at the exact moment they most bind. Petrov survived his 1983 event because a slow correct instinct beat a fast confident wrong system; the Petrov failure mode inverted is the case where the slow instinct never gets called.

## Belief

The load-bearing shift on the cyber and NC3 channel is not "AI enables a disarming cyber strike." It is that AI progress shifts the target's THREAT MODEL on classical NC3 assumptions across the board, and cannot be verified from the outside. Airgap, custom hardware, physical isolation, hardware-random authentication, human-in-loop verification: each was a reliable assumption a decade ago; each is now conditional on the adversary's AI capability, which is unknown even to the adversary within tight bounds. The response to unverifiable erosion of an assumption is doctrine-level, not technical. Chinese planners cannot patch away Mythos-class exposure risk, because they cannot bound it. They can harden individual components and they can reduce their reliance on the assumption. Reducing reliance on the assumption means launch-on-warning, pre-delegation, dead-hand: all the same commitment machinery from the decision-channel section, arriving via a different door.

The stability consequence follows directly from Acton's entanglement work with an AI accelerant applied. A target of persistent access cannot distinguish espionage from attack-preparation and cannot distinguish disarming cyber first strike from decapitation cyber operation until too late to distinguish safely. AI compresses the timescale on which the distinction would resolve. That is not a claim about whether the intrusion was ever successful. It is a claim about what the target believes about the intrusion's plausibility, which is the strategic input.

Stuxnet's actual lesson to adversaries was harden, diversify, assume compromise. It also produced use-it-or-lose-it pressure on any arsenal or system whose survival requires a specific technical assumption the adversary is believed to have breached. Cyber counterforce is destabilizing precisely because states will believe it might work whether or not it does. If the belief is common knowledge, and the response is doctrine-level (LOW plus pre-delegation), and the response feeds the decision-channel compression, the three channels reinforce each other. That reinforcement is my strongest single objection to the FA weighting.

## Prize

The fourth channel is not in the FA taxonomy and is not in most stability analyses, and I think it is where the essay's original contribution lives. AI is not only a capability that acts on the strategic balance. It is a STAKE that changes the payoff matrix. That framing generates a class of destabilization scenarios that operate at the conventional level and reach nuclear stability through escalation pathways, not through direct action on the nuclear balance itself.

Compute is the bottleneck resource for AI capability. Leading-node fabrication is the bottleneck for compute. Leading-node fabs are physically concentrated: TSMC Taiwan primary; TSMC Arizona Phase 1 in limited production at 4 nm with Phase 2 under construction; Samsung and Intel trailing on execution; SMIC pushing 7 nm without EUV via heroic multi-patterning. The state of play in July 2026 as I read it is that neither superpower has domestic self-sufficiency at the AI-relevant node, both are running hard, and the ordering of who crosses first is genuinely uncertain and depends on ASML export enforcement, TSMC ramp discipline, and Intel's 18A execution. I want to build the argument in a form that does not require getting that ordering right, because the stability-relevant dynamic is the ASYMMETRIC CLOSING-WINDOW structure, not the outcome.

Two windows. China's window to act on Taiwan before domestic leading-node self-sufficiency, during which TSMC is a Chinese strategic asset (both as prize if taken intact and as leverage if denied to the US) and destruction is undesirable. And US window to deter action on Taiwan before US domestic fab ramp reduces the cost of losing TSMC access, during which credibility of extended deterrence toward Taipei remains high. Both windows close. The dangerous regime is when they close on different timelines; the side whose window closes later has strong incentive to act while the other side's leverage is still binding.

Once Beijing achieves domestic capability, the payoff matrix on Taiwan inverts in a specific way that Dan flagged and that I would not have arrived at independently. TSMC intact benefits Beijing marginally after indigenization, since they no longer NEED TSMC. TSMC intact benefits Washington strongly, since the US ramp is incomplete. So TSMC destroyed is a good Beijing outcome relative to TSMC-in-US-hands, and the calculus about engineering-talent loss becomes cavalier: destroying the physical fabs and the engineering base is mostly access-denial against the US, which is a legitimate Chinese war aim once nationalist reunification is the primary driver. The "silicon shield" classical assumption breaks. Shield-value assumed both parties inherit TSMC if they win. That assumption is conditional on both parties still needing it.

The US side of this generates a Schelling commitment paradox. Every US measure that visibly hedges against TSMC loss (airlift planning, Arizona ramp acceleration, allied fab diversification) reduces vulnerability and reduces credibility to defend Taiwan simultaneously, because visible hedging signals reduced willingness to bear the cost. The US cannot credibly commit to defend Taiwan AND credibly hedge against losing TSMC at the same time. Every hedge move is read in Beijing as a credibility discount on the commitment.

The escalation chain to nuclear stability runs through conventional-nuclear entanglement. A full Taiwan crisis draws in US carrier strike groups and USAF assets. Chinese DF-21D and DF-26 are dual-capable conventional-nuclear; some US assets sit inside SIOP; potential JASSM and LRHW strike packages may or may not carry nuclear ambiguity depending on adversary discrimination. Acton's entanglement problem gets acute. Any one of these ambiguity-loaded exchanges is a candidate escalation trigger. The nuclear-stability assumption stressed is the deepest one: "great powers avoid direct conflict because nuclear risk exceeds any conventional prize." Historically no conventional prize was worth the risk, and this assumption was stable because it was true. AGI plausibly is worth the risk, or is believed to be worth the risk in Beijing and in Washington, which is analytically the same thing. That is the load-bearing assumption breakdown the prize channel produces.

I should note where I pressure-test my own argument. Chinese calculus for Taiwan is primarily nationalist: One China ideology, domestic political legitimacy, Xi's stated commitment to reunification as a marker of his own historical standing. The much-cited 2027 date is a US analytic construct (the "Davidson window" from Adm. Philip Davidson's March 2021 SASC testimony) reinforced by CIA-attributed reporting in February 2023 that Xi ordered PLA readiness by 2027; Burns immediately qualified that a readiness order is not an invasion decision, and Beijing has not publicly confirmed a date. Recent PLA leadership purges (Zhang Youxia and Liu Zhenli from the Central Military Commission in early 2026, apparently over disagreements about invasion feasibility and Xi's reform plans) suggest a slippage in whatever internal timeline exists, and 83% of China analysts surveyed by CSIS reject the view that Beijing plans kinetic action against Taiwan by 2027. Fabs are a marginal accelerant on a pressure system that already existed, not the driver. The essay should be honest about that ordering and argue that the AI race adds a new accelerant to nationalist pressure, not that it invents the pressure. I do think it is a large accelerant under Chang's operative clause, but I want that concession on the record.

## Comparison

FA's core observations at the capability layer are correct and the essay concedes them. Splendid first strike remains infeasible at any credible probability of success. C3 elimination in one fell swoop is not tractable. Missile defense will not close the gap alone. Any survey that argues against these is arguing against physics. My critique is that FA's argument works by comparing capability against a bright line (unqualified splendid first strike, complete C3 elimination, invulnerable defense) and finding capability below the line, then treating the space between the line and current capability as a stability zone. It is a threshold argument that never names its threshold, and stability lives well below any such line.

FA underweights the perception channel, treating it as a caveat when it is the load-bearing thesis for a taxonomy question. The specific quoted concession, "even if predictions of AI power overestimate the technology's actual capabilities, states may perceive greater threats and take potentially destabilizing actions," is exactly right and immediately shelved. Under Chang's operative clause it should be the framing, not the caveat.

FA misses three entries that this taxonomy carries: the prize channel entirely, the sudden-takeoff regime hedged in a single sentence, and the asymmetric erosion structure that generates structurally different US-vs-China threat pictures from the same technology. On asymmetric erosion in particular: the average destabilization is small, and average is the wrong metric for stability questions where the tail dominates.

FA's strongest point, worth conceding directly: stability logic is deeper than any single technology, and "AI transformation" as a phrase does too much analytical work in the discourse. That is correct and the taxonomy above is built to respect it. FA's weakest point, worth pressing directly: their analysis is calibrated to the pre-takeoff regime. The meta-model assumes both sides have decades to adapt to new tech, and that assumption fails first under Chang's clause.

## Takeoff

FA's one-sentence hedge on rapid capability takeoff is the entry the whole taxonomy pivots through if the hedge fires. Time-to-adaptation collapses. Offense-defense balance can shift within crisis-relevant timescales. The wonder-weapon pathologies of the Kahn-Payne era become operationally relevant in a form Schelling did not anticipate: an opponent's belief that you have unmatchable capability drives preemption incentive even if you do not, and the belief formation timescale under takeoff is faster than the arms-control-negotiation timescale that would normally correct it. MAD's classical mutual-knowledge-of-mutual-vulnerability assumption breaks decisively, because mutual knowledge collapses when the progress rate exceeds the verification rate.

## Close

Nuclear stability was always epistemic and doctrinal, not purely technical. Schelling knew it and stated it repeatedly, and the FA analysis is at bottom a reassurance that the physics-locks remain in place. The physics-locks do remain in place, and they protect capabilities, and stability is not a capability. AI attacks the epistemic and doctrinal foundations directly through all four channels above, and the FA weighting assumes those foundations are more robust to information-technology attack than any of Schelling's original conditions suggest they are.

What would change my mind: explicit no-first-use commitments matched by observable force posture; verifiable de-alerting across the P5 arsenals; arms-control instruments specifically on AI in NC3 decision-support functions; visible slowing of the fab-race window-closing dynamics through a US-China compute compact or equivalent. Absent all four, the four channels are active and reinforcing, and the load-bearing assumptions Chang's question asks after are breaking one at a time.
