## Why acoustics is the real SSBN question, and why the satellite section wasn't enough

The satellite analysis establishes that no overhead sensor sees a hull at patrol depth, so the constellation attacks the submarine only through surface expressions that fall off as depth-cubed, and its most useful role is cueing: shrink the search box, hand the box to something that can actually localize. The something is almost always acoustic. So the load-bearing question for the sea leg is not "can a satellite find the boat" (it can't, directly) but "does AI on acoustic arrays turn a shrunken search box into a kill-quality track." If it does, the constellation plus acoustic-ML together close what neither closes alone, and the FA authors' "oceans won't go transparent" is too comfortable. If it doesn't, the sea leg holds where the geography permits, and the whole essay's asymmetry conclusion rests on this section rather than on the Bernoulli scaling.

I think the honest answer is that AI moves the acoustic contest meaningfully but asymmetrically, in the same direction the satellite analysis already found (against the noisy boat in the bastion, not the quiet boat in open ocean), and that the reason is legible in the sonar equation rather than a matter of taste.

## The passive sonar equation is where the argument has to live

Passive detection is a signal-excess bookkeeping problem. In the standard form, a contact is detectable when

    SE = SL − TL − (NL − AG) − DT ≥ 0

source level minus transmission loss minus noise (ambient, reduced by the array gain the receiver extracts) minus the detection threshold the processor needs. "AI finds the subs" is a claim about exactly two of these terms, and it is worth being precise about which, because the other three are where the physics lives.

**AI does not touch SL or TL.** Source level is the boat's radiated noise, set by the adversary's own quieting engineering: hull treatments, raft-mounted machinery, prop design, and above all speed, because flow noise and cavitation rise steeply with it. Transmission loss is ocean acoustics: spreading, absorption, and the sound-speed structure (thermocline, convergence zones, the deep sound channel) that decides whether energy even reaches the receiver. These are the SL and TL analogues of the satellite section's kinematic bin, and they are the terms the evader controls. A boat that goes slow drops its source level; a boat that chooses its depth and water mass chooses its transmission loss. Neither is a processing problem, and no amount of ML on the receiver side puts energy into the water that the boat didn't radiate or recovers energy the ocean already absorbed.

**AI moves AG and DT, and this is real.** Array gain is how much ambient noise the receiver can reject by exploiting the spatial and temporal structure of the field, and detection threshold is how much signal excess the processor needs to call a contact. This is the acoustic analogue of the processing bin, and it is a genuine machine-learning target: modern methods do better than classical beamforming and matched-field processing at pulling a structured signal out of a structured, non-stationary background, at classifying a contact once detected, and at fusing intermittent detections across a distributed field into a track (track-before-detect, which integrates signal excess that is individually sub-threshold). The distributed-sensor economics the satellite section invoked apply here too: cheap unmanned platforms (gliders, unmanned surface vessels, deployable field arrays) multiply the number of look-geometries, and multiplying geometry is exactly what defeats an accrual limit. So the searcher's dial is real. It is just, once again, a linear-to-modest dial against terms the evader moves multiplicatively.

## What ML can and cannot exploit: the tonal-vs-broadband split

The sharpest way to state the limit is in terms of what signal structure exists to be exploited. Passive ASW historically lived on narrowband tonals: discrete frequency lines from rotating machinery and blade rate, which stand up above broadband ambient because their energy is concentrated and their frequencies are stable enough to integrate coherently over long dwell. This is where classical processing already worked, and where ML's marginal gain over a matched filter is smallest, because a matched filter against a known line is already near-optimal. Modern quieting is a direct attack on this channel: isolate the machinery, and the tonals that made the boat legible sink beneath ambient, at which point there is no line for either a matched filter or a neural net to integrate. The evader doesn't out-compute the searcher; he deletes the feature.

What remains is broadband and transient structure, and this is where ML genuinely earns its place: a learned model can exploit subtle spectral shape, modulation, and multi-sensor coherence that hand-built detectors miss. But two things bound it, and both are physics, not pessimism. First, the exploitable structure has to be above ambient somewhere in the field to be learned from at detection time, and quieting plus slow-speed operation drives it toward the ambient floor, which is itself rising (shipping, and increasingly the same proliferation of unmanned platforms, raise background noise). Second, and this is the term a grader will press on, a learned classifier's operating point is set by the same near-zero-false-alarm tyranny as the boost-phase problem, transposed to the sea: the ocean is full of biologics, merchants, and geologic noise, false contacts are the normal case, and a hunt-to-kill track against a nuclear submarine cannot run at a false-alarm rate that floods the shooter or, worse, prosecutes a neutral. ML shifts the ROC curve; it does not repeal the requirement that the operating point sit where false alarms are rare against a background that is heavy-tailed and, near chokepoints, adversarially populated.

## The asymmetry survives, and it survives for the same reason

Put the terms together by regime, because the strategic conclusion is regime-dependent and that is the whole point.

A quiet boat, slow, in deep open ocean with favorable sound-speed structure: low SL, high and variable TL, tonals below ambient, sparse look-geometry because the search area is enormous. Every term the evader controls is set to punishing, and ML is working the two terms that help least against a boat that has zeroed the features they'd exploit. This is the American Trident regime, and it is why "transparent oceans" overclaims: the processing revolution is real and still loses here, because SL and TL dominate and the evader owns them.

A noisier boat operating from a bastion with constrained approaches, transiting predictable chokepoints, in water a defender has pre-instrumented with fixed and deployable arrays: higher SL, shorter and better-characterized TL, denser look-geometry, and a defender who can concentrate exactly the distributed-sensor-plus-fusion architecture ML most rewards. This is the Chinese 094 regime, and it is where acoustic-ML plus satellite cueing genuinely compound: the satellite shrinks the box to the chokepoint, the pre-laid field plus fusion holds the track through it. The ocean does not go transparent; it goes translucent exactly where the weaker deterrent has to swim.

So the acoustic analysis strengthens rather than rescues the draft's conclusion, and it does so from the sonar equation rather than by assertion: AI moves AG and DT for both sides, but the terms that decide the contest (SL via quieting, TL via geography) are the evader's, and only one of the two navies is positioned to use them. The FA authors are right that the sea won't go transparent and wrong to read that as symmetric reassurance. The correct claim is narrower and worse: translucency is regime-selective, it selects against China first, and China knows it, which is one more input feeding the buildup, the bastion investments, and the slide toward launch-on-warning that the rest of the essay tracks.

## Honest limits of this section

I have reasoned from the sonar equation and the tonal-vs-broadband structure, not from current detection ranges, which are classified and which I will not invent. Two things would move this analysis, and both belong in the update criteria: published or leaked evidence that learned processing achieves useful detection ranges against a quieted boat at low speed (which would mean ML is reaching broadband structure I've assumed stays near ambient), or a demonstrated wide-area fusion capability that holds track on a quiet boat across open ocean rather than only through a cued chokepoint. I expect neither, because both require beating SL and TL rather than AG and DT, but I have not run a quantitative range calculation and I am not going to pretend a back-of-envelope on classified quieting figures would be credible.
