# MAD about AI

**Recommendation:** The State Department's Bureau of Arms Control, Nonproliferation, and Stability should codify the November 2024 Biden–Xi affirmation of human control over the decision to use nuclear weapons.  The affirmation is currently declaratory-only, and remains undefined and unverified.  The affirmation should be negotiated into a USA-China framework of AI in nuclear decisions, which would then ideally be extended to the P5.  

Schelling argued that surrendering control to an automated system makes the threat of retaliation more credible.  Kavka had noted that a rational agent can't sincerely intend to follow through on a retaliation that doesn't prevent any damage and only kills millions.  For years we have tried to resolve that paradox through a combination of training, loyalty, and duty.  But on at least two occasions (Petrov and Arkhipov), humans have overridden procedures that would have (mistakenly!) produced a retaliatory strike.  While we now, with the benefit of hindsight, consider those decisions to have been correct, they nevertheless weaken the credibility of second-strike retaliation that supports MAD as a whole.

So from the perspective of defensive posture, those were reliability failures.  A natural replacement would be AI which could autonomously respond, since a machine can follow orders without guilt.  This immediately produces a tension between deterrence and corrigibility.  Every alignment desideratum (corrigibility, interruptibility, a reliable off-switch, human-in-the-loop, predictability) is *anti*-commitment. The ideal agent is docile at baseline but absolutely inexorable once an adversary crosses the line. Posture designers chasing credibility will spec systems that safety engineers are trying to make impossible.  And the reasons we want corrigibility are not academic: the field's own leaders rank losing control of AI alongside nuclear war (Statement on AI Risk, 2023).  Pre-delegated retaliation is where those two risks become one system.

Superhuman AI hacking abilities (such as those found in Mythos) accelerate the NC3 fear cycle.  The USA doesn't have to succeed in compromising China's systems; the mere knowledge that said hacker exists, and that even air-gapped systems can be compromised (e.g. Stuxnet), can be destabilizing on its own.  The fact that NC3 shares networks with conventional C2 makes espionage almost indistinguishable from disarming-strike prep, and a command system that believes the channels may be compromised will have a strong incentive to pre-delegate retaliation decisions to machines that can't be recalled.

The Biden-Xi statement is a good starting point to build on.  But we need to create definitions, scope, and verification paths soon; otherwise force-modernization decisions on both sides will close the option within a few years.  Currently, nobody is negotiating it.
 


==================


**The problem is commitment logic.** 


Schelling showed that surrendering control makes threats credible; Kavka noted that rational agents cannot sincerely intend retaliation that saves nothing and kills millions. The Cold War bridged the paradox with training and duty — but Arkhipov reconsidered, and from the posture designer's chair he was a reliability failure. Moscow's answer was Perimeter. AI is the natural extension: it resolves Kavka's paradox because the machine doesn't need to *want* retaliation; it only needs to lack the ability to change its mind.

The alignment literature calls that ability corrigibility. Every alignment desideratum — interruptibility, human-in-the-loop, a reliable off-switch — is anti-commitment.  To at least one important customer, a corrigible AI is a defective product. That is not a failure mode markets or doctrine will self-correct; it is what the incentives, uncorrected, produce.

**The spiral is already loading.** AI accelerates the offensive side of the NC3 fear cycle. It doesn't have to pwn an adversary's NC3; Beijing only has to worry that it might have, and cannot prove otherwise — absence-of-intrusion is exactly what you cannot verify. Entanglement makes it worse: NC3 shares sensors and networks with conventional C2, so espionage is indistinguishable from disarming-strike prep (Acton 2018). A commander who believes the channel may be compromised faces pressure to pre-delegate — to machines whose selling point is that they cannot be recalled.

**Why State, why this seam.** The Biden–Xi statement is a live diplomatic hook that costs nothing to build on and is worth nothing until someone does: no definitions, no scope, no verification path. Force-modernization decisions on both sides will foreclose the option space within a few years.

NC3-AI integration is where arms control has to move next, and nobody is negotiating it. The Department can be first.
